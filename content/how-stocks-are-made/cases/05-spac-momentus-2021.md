A **SPAC** (special purpose acquisition company) is a listed shell company that raises money first and then looks for a private company to merge with. In **July 2021**, the SEC charged the SPAC **Stable Road Acquisition Company**, its sponsor and CEO, its planned merger target, the space transportation company **Momentus**, and Momentus's founder and former CEO.

According to the SEC's settled order:

- Momentus and its former CEO repeatedly told investors that its propulsion technology had been "successfully tested" in space, although its only in-space test had **failed** to achieve its primary mission objectives.
- They also misrepresented how far **national security concerns** about the former CEO threatened the licences Momentus needed.
- Stable Road **repeated** these misleading statements in its public filings and **failed its due diligence**: it never reviewed the results of the in-space test.

The settling parties agreed to total penalties of **more than $8 million**, and the sponsor was to forfeit founder's shares it stood to receive from the merger. The SEC's case against the former CEO went to court. The settling parties neither admitted nor denied the findings.

**What it shows:** the SEC's then-chair said the case illustrated risks inherent to SPACs, because those who stand to earn significant profits from a SPAC merger may do inadequate due diligence and mislead investors. In a traditional IPO, underwriters carry out due diligence before the offer (step 5). In a SPAC, investors depend heavily on the sponsors, whose interests can differ from their own.
