The Dutch East India Company (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie, VOC) was founded in 1602. Euronext, which runs the Amsterdam exchange today, describes the VOC as the first company to go to the exchange in 1602, and so as the founder of the Amsterdam stock exchange. It also credits the VOC with an earlier innovation: the modern limited liability company.

**What it shows:** the basic idea has not changed in more than 400 years. A company sells ownership to many investors to finance a large, risky venture, and those investors can later sell their stakes to others on an exchange instead of waiting for the company to pay them back.
