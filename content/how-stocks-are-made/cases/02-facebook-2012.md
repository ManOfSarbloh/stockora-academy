Facebook's shares started trading on Nasdaq on **18 May 2012**, in what was expected to be one of the largest IPOs in history. According to the SEC, a **design limitation** in Nasdaq's system for matching IPO buy and sell orders (the opening "cross") caused the cross to fall **19 minutes** behind the orders it received. More than **38,000** orders were left out of the cross, and about **30,000** of them were stuck in Nasdaq's system for more than two hours.

Nasdaq's senior staff decided to start trading anyway, believing they had fixed the problem, without understanding its root cause. In **May 2013** the SEC charged Nasdaq with rule violations, and Nasdaq agreed to pay a **$10 million** penalty, which the SEC called the largest ever against an exchange. Nasdaq settled without admitting or denying the findings.

**What it shows:** listing day depends on technology as well as on demand. The opening auction (step 7) has to collect and match a huge number of orders, and when it fails, investors may not know for hours whether their orders were executed.
