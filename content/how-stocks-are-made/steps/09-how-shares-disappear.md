### What happens

- **Takeover or merger:** another company buys the shares, paying in cash, its own shares or both.
- **Squeeze-out:** a majority owner forces out the remaining small shareholders against cash compensation.
- **Going private:** investors buy all the shares and take the company off the exchange.
- **Delisting:** the shares leave the exchange but still exist; selling them becomes much harder.
- **Bankruptcy:** creditors are paid first; ordinary shareholders often receive nothing.

### Who is involved

Buyers and bidders, the board, shareholders voting at the general meeting, independent experts, courts and, in a bankruptcy, the insolvency administrator.

### Example with numbers

> [!EXAMPLE]
> After a takeover offer, a group owns **92%** of an Austrian AG and squeezes out the remaining **8%**. You own 100 shares: you cannot refuse, but you receive cash compensation for each share, checked by an independent expert.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> Under the **Gesellschafter-Ausschlussgesetz**, a shareholder with at least **90%** of the capital can have the general meeting transfer the remaining shares to them, against appropriate cash compensation.

> [!WARNING]
> In a bankruptcy, shareholders are last in line. Owning shares means you can lose everything you invested, but not more.
