### What happens

After the IPO, the company is no longer involved when its shares change hands. Investors trade with each other on the **secondary market**. Their buy and sell orders are collected in the exchange's **order book** and matched by **price-time priority**: the best price first, and at the same price, the earliest order first. At set times, **auctions** collect orders and find a single price at which the most shares can trade.

Prices move with supply and demand: company news and earnings, interest rates, the economy and the mood of the market.

### Who is involved

Private and institutional investors, brokers, market makers and the exchange's matching engine.

### Example with numbers

> [!EXAMPLE]
> The best buy order in the book is **€20.10** and the best sell order **€20.20**. You send a market order to buy: it trades at **€20.20**, against the cheapest seller. The gap of 10 cents is the **bid-ask spread**.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> On the Vienna Stock Exchange, ATX shares have an opening auction (08:55–09:00), continuous trading with a short intraday auction around midday, and a closing auction (17:30–17:35). Check the exchange's trading-hours page for current times.
