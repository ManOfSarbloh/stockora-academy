### What happens

A listed company lives in public. It publishes regular financial reports, announces price-relevant news promptly, and holds a **general meeting** every year where shareholders vote. It may pay **dividends**, **buy back** its own shares, or issue **new shares** (for example in a **rights issue**, where existing shareholders may buy first). Index providers may add it to, or remove it from, their indexes.

### Who is involved

Management, the supervisory board, shareholders, auditors, the regulator, analysts and index providers.

### Example with numbers

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A company has **10 million shares** and you own **100,000** (1%). It issues **2 million** new shares in a rights issue. If you do not take part, you still own 100,000 shares, but now of 12 million: **0.83%**.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> In the EU, listed companies publish **annual and half-yearly financial reports**, and price-relevant news as **ad-hoc announcements**. The FMA supervises these duties.

> [!NOTE] In the United States
> Companies file annual reports on **Form 10-K**, quarterly reports on **Form 10-Q** for the first three quarters of their fiscal year, and current reports on **Form 8-K**, generally within four business days of certain major events.
