### What happens

Before trading starts, the share gets an **ISIN** (its international ID) and a **ticker** from the exchange. The whole issue is recorded at a **central securities depository (CSD)** as an electronic **book entry**, often backed by one global certificate. Nothing is printed for individual investors.

When you buy, the shares travel down a **chain of accounts**: you → your broker → the broker's custodian bank → the CSD → the issuing company. For registered shares, the company also keeps a **share register**.

### Who is involved

The national numbering agency, the CSD, custodian banks, brokers and the company (or its registrar).

### Example with numbers

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You buy **50 shares**. After settlement, your broker's statement shows 50 shares in your securities account, and at the CSD the account of your broker's custodian rises by 50 shares, together with all its other clients' holdings.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> ISINs are assigned by the **OeKB**, and the CSD is **OeKB CSD**, a subsidiary of the OeKB.

> [!NOTE] In the United States
> The central securities depository is the **Depository Trust Company (DTC)**.
