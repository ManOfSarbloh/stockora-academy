Good habits matter more than any single tool (Category O). This lesson puts the earlier lessons of this category into a routine you can repeat.

## When you hear about an opportunity

1. **Who is offering it?** Check the provider in the **FMA company database** and the **FMA warning list** (Category T). If it comes via a chat group or a stranger online, stop here.
2. **What is it, exactly?** Find the ISIN and the product type: share, ETF, certificate, CFD (Categories A, H, N).
3. **Is it news, opinion or advertising?** (lesson 3)
4. **What does the original say?** Read the company announcement, report or key information document, not only the summary (lesson 2).
5. **Does it fit your plan?** Compare with your goals, time horizon and risk tolerance (Category M).
6. **Wait a day.** Urgency is a fraud red flag and a source of bias (Categories O and T).

## When you look at a company

| Step | What to look at | Category |
|---|---|---|
| Business | What does it sell, to whom, and how does it make money? | I |
| Numbers | Sales, profit, cash flow, debt over several years | I |
| Valuation | P/E, dividend yield, compared with history and peers | I, G |
| Events | Upcoming results, general meeting, corporate actions | R, V |
| Price | Chart for context, not for prediction | J |
| Risks | What could go wrong? How large a position? | P |

## Keep a record

Write down **why** you bought, what you expected and what would make you sell. Later, compare outcome with reasoning. A decision diary protects against hindsight ("I knew it all along") and self-attribution (Category O), and good records also help with taxes (Category Q).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Start with who is offering something; check the FMA database and warning list.
> - Identify the exact product, the type of content, and the original source.
> - Look at business, numbers, valuation, events, price and risks, in that order.
> - Keep a written record of your reasons and expectations.
> - Use the glossary, trading times, Chart School and search to support each step.
