Every broker app, exchange website and financial news site shows a **quote page** for each share. The layout differs, but the building blocks are the same.

## The core figures

The Wiener Börse explains the figures in a typical price table:

| Figure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Name, ticker, ISIN** | Which security this is. The ISIN is unique; tickers differ between exchanges (Category A). |
| **Market segment** | The segment where the share is admitted, based on criteria such as market capitalisation and liquidity (Category D). |
| **High / Low / Close** | The highest, lowest and closing prices of the day. |
| **Previous H/L/C** | The same figures for the previous trading day. |
| **+/− %** | Change in the closing price from the previous day, in percent. |
| **Volume (shares)** | Number of shares traded that day. |
| **Turnover (€ million)** | Value traded: volume × closing price. |
| **P/E ratio** | How many times the earnings per share are contained in the price (Category I). |
| **Free float** | Share of the shares in circulation on the exchange (Category C). |

Online quote pages usually add:

- **Bid and ask:** the best prices at which others are currently willing to buy and sell, and the **spread** between them (Category D).
- **Market capitalisation:** share price × number of shares (Category I).
- **52-week high and low:** the range over the past year.

## Delayed or real time?

Free price data is often **delayed**. The Wiener Börse's own website, for example, shows prices **15 minutes delayed**, with the ATX in real time. A quote page should say which applies; look for "delayed", a time stamp or "real time".

> [!WARNING] Don't trade on a stale price
> A delayed price can be out of date in a fast market. Before placing an order, check the live bid and ask in your broker's order screen, and consider a limit order (Category F).

## Codes next to the price

Newspapers and price lists add short codes (*Kurszusätze*). Two common ones in Austria (Wiener Börse):

- **eD**, *ex Dividende*: on the **ex-date**, the price was reduced by the dividend (Category G).
- **eB**, *ex Bezugsrecht*: the value of the **subscription right** was deducted from the price (Category V).

Both explain price drops that are **not losses** for existing shareholders.

> [!EXAMPLE] Reading one line (fictional)
> Alpenrad AG · Prime Market · H/L/C 31.40 / 30.10 / 30.20 eD · −3.8% · Volume 182,000 · Turnover €5.5 million
>
> The share closed at €30.20, down 3.8%, but "eD" shows that it went ex-dividend that day. With a dividend of €1.10, most of the fall is the dividend deduction, not a loss.

## Same share, different numbers

You may see slightly different prices for the same share on different sites. Reasons include:

- different **exchanges or trading venues** (Category D);
- **currency**: the same company quoted in euros on one venue and dollars on another;
- **delayed** versus real-time data;
- **adjusted** versus unadjusted history after splits or dividends (Categories J and V).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - High/low/close, change, volume and turnover describe the trading day.
> - Bid, ask and spread show what you could trade at right now.
> - Free data is often delayed; the Wiener Börse website shows prices 15 minutes delayed.
> - "eD" and "eB" mark ex-dividend and ex-rights price deductions, not losses.
> - Prices for the same share can differ by venue, currency, delay and adjustment.
