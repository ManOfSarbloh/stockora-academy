A **central bank** issues a country's or a currency area's money and steers its interest rates. For investors in Austria, the most important ones are the European Central Bank (ECB) and, because US markets are so large, the US Federal Reserve (Fed).

## The ECB and the Eurosystem

Austria uses the euro, so its interest rates are set for the whole euro area by the **ECB Governing Council**. The ECB and the national central banks of the euro area countries together form the **Eurosystem**.

> [!AUSTRIA] The role of the Oesterreichische Nationalbank (OeNB)
> The OeNB is Austria's central bank and part of the Eurosystem. According to the OeNB, its **governor is Austria's representative on the ECB Governing Council**, where monetary policy decisions for the euro area are taken jointly. The OeNB helps implement those decisions in Austria and also contributes to financial stability.

### The goal: 2% inflation over the medium term

The ECB's primary objective is **price stability**. Its strategy says the Governing Council aims for **2% inflation over the medium term**, measured by the **Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP)**.

- The target is **symmetric**: inflation that is too low is considered as undesirable as inflation that is too high.
- "Medium term" allows short-term deviations, because monetary policy works with long and variable delays (lesson 3).

### The three key ECB interest rates

| Rate | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Deposit facility rate** | What banks earn on overnight deposits with the Eurosystem. Since March 2024 the ECB steers its policy stance through this rate. |
| **Main refinancing operations rate** | What banks pay to borrow from the ECB on a weekly basis against collateral. Set above the deposit rate. |
| **Marginal lending facility rate** | What banks pay for overnight credit. Set above the main refinancing rate. |

When news reports say "the ECB raised rates", they now usually mean the deposit facility rate.

> [!EXAMPLE] A decade of extremes
> From the ECB's own table of key interest rates (effective dates):
> - **June 2014:** the deposit rate went **below zero** for the first time (−0.10%). Banks paid to park money at the central bank.
> - **September 2019:** it reached **−0.50%**.
> - **July 2022 to September 2023:** ten increases took it from −0.50% to **4.00%**, as inflation surged.
> - **June 2024 to June 2025:** a series of cuts took it back to **2.00%**.

> [!VERIFY] Current rates
> Interest rates change several times a year. Check the current key rates on the ECB's "Key ECB interest rates" page, which lists every change with its effective date.

## The US Federal Reserve

The Fed's goals are set by the US Congress. Its FAQ describes a **dual mandate**: **maximum employment** and **stable prices**. The Fed's Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) judges that **2% inflation** over the longer run, measured by the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE), is most consistent with price stability.

| | ECB | Fed |
|---|---|---|
| Decision body | Governing Council | Federal Open Market Committee |
| Main goal | Price stability (primary objective) | Maximum employment **and** stable prices |
| Inflation target | 2% (HICP), medium term, symmetric | 2% (PCE), longer run |

## Why investors watch them

- Their decisions move **interest rates**, which affect share and bond prices (lesson 1).
- Their **statements about the future** (forward guidance, Category K) can move markets even when rates do not change.
- In crises they act as a **lender of last resort** and can buy bonds on a large scale (Category K).

> [!MISTAKE] Thinking the ECB targets the stock market
> The ECB's objective is price stability, not share prices. Share prices are one of the channels through which its policy works, but it does not aim to keep markets rising.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Austria's interest rates are set for the whole euro area by the ECB Governing Council; the OeNB governor is a member.
> - The ECB aims for 2% HICP inflation over the medium term, symmetrically.
> - The deposit facility rate is now the ECB's main policy rate.
> - The Fed has a dual mandate: maximum employment and stable prices (2% PCE inflation).
> - Check current rates on the ECB's website; they change often.
