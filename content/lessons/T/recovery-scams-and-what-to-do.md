Being defrauded is distressing, and many victims feel ashamed. Fraudsters are professionals; what matters now is acting quickly to limit the damage.

## What to do immediately

The FMA advises anyone who suspects they have been a victim of investment fraud to:

1. **Contact your own bank immediately.**
2. **Report it to the police** (*Anzeige erstatten*).
3. **Report the suspicious profiles** to the platforms where they appear, as scam or fraud attempts.

The FMA stresses that acting quickly can help prevent further damage. In addition:

- **Stop all contact** and **send no more money** (Investor.gov).
- **Secure your accounts:** change passwords, and if you installed remote access software, have your device checked and remove it.
- **Keep evidence:** messages, emails, website addresses, transfer receipts, wallet addresses.
- If an FMA-supervised company is involved, you can also contact the **FMA** (Category R).

## The second wave: recovery scams

Victims are often targeted **again**. Investor.gov describes **reload scams**: scammers target previous victims and may **impersonate government regulators** or other organisations, claiming the victim's funds have been or can be **recovered**, in exchange for an upfront fee. In relationship scams, after breaking off contact, the scammer may reach out again pretending to be a regulator, a law firm or another entity that has supposedly recovered the funds, sending phony paperwork and demanding payment.

> [!WARNING] Never pay to get your money back
> Investor.gov's advice is clear: never pay money to recover your investment or make an upfront payment to release funds. The FMA warns that fraudsters also pose as FMA staff with forged documents; genuine FMA email addresses end only in @fma.gv.at.

> [!EXAMPLE] A typical recovery offer
> "We are an international asset recovery agency working with the FMA. We have located your €25,000. To release it, please pay a processing fee of €2,500." This is another scam: regulators do not charge victims fees to return money.

## Talk about it

Telling family or friends can feel hard, but it helps: they can support you, and warning others makes fraud harder. Fraudsters rely on silence.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Contact your bank immediately, report to the police and report the fraudsters' profiles (FMA).
> - Stop all contact and payments, secure your accounts and keep evidence.
> - Expect "recovery" offers from fake lawyers, agencies or regulators: they are scams.
> - Never pay a fee to recover money or release funds.
> - Talking about it protects you and others.
