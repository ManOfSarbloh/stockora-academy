## The chat-group scam

The FMA reports that investment fraud via **messenger services** such as WhatsApp and Telegram is **increasing**. The typical pattern:

1. You are **invited into a chat group**, often after an ad, a social media contact or a "wrong number" message.
2. Supposed investors in the group report **high profits**, and self-proclaimed **financial experts** give investment recommendations.
3. You are encouraged to open an account on a trading platform or app, often with a small first amount.
4. Early "profits" appear on screen. You are encouraged to invest more (lesson 6).

> [!WARNING] The other members may not be real
> In such groups, the enthusiastic "investors" can be fake accounts or accomplices. The FMA notes that fraudsters use AI-generated images, videos and voices to create credible identities. Social proof that looks real may be manufactured.

## Pump and dump

Investor.gov describes **pump-and-dump** schemes in two parts:

1. **Pump:** promoters try to boost the price of a share with **false or misleading statements** about the company, often online, urging readers to **buy quickly**, sometimes claiming to have "inside" information.
2. **Dump:** once the price has risen, they **sell their own shares**. When they stop hyping the share, the price typically **falls**, and investors lose money.

Such schemes often involve small, thinly traded shares, where a little buying moves the price a lot (Category C). In the EU and Austria, spreading false or misleading information to move prices is **market manipulation** and is punishable (Category R).

> [!EXAMPLE] How the latecomers lose
> A promoter buys 1 million shares at €0.10. The group is told about a "secret breakthrough". Members buy, pushing the price to €0.40. The promoter sells everything at around €0.40, earning about €300,000. Buying dries up, and the price falls back towards €0.10, or lower. The members who bought at €0.30–0.40 lose most of their money.

## Social media stock tips and finfluencers

Not every tip is fraud, but the FMA advises checking who is behind a recommendation, their qualifications and whether they have a **financial interest**, for example through affiliate marketing (Category O). "Finfluencer" is not a protected title, and there are fraudsters among them.

## What to do

- **Leave** groups that promise high returns or push specific shares.
- **Never** act on "insider" tips; trading on real inside information is illegal, and fake ones are a lure (Category R).
- **Check** any platform or firm with the Scam Shield checks (lesson 3).
- **Report** suspicious profiles to the platform; the FMA recommends this too.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - The FMA reports that investment fraud via WhatsApp and Telegram groups is increasing.
> - Group members reporting big profits may be fake; AI makes identities look real.
> - Pump-and-dump: hype a cheap share with misleading claims, then sell into the rise.
> - Spreading false information to move prices is market manipulation, punishable in Austria.
> - Leave such groups, ignore "insider" tips, check firms and report suspicious profiles.
