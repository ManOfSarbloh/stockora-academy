## The scale in Austria

In a press release of 30 September 2026, the Austrian Financial Market Authority (FMA) reported that in **2025 alone** it received **843 reports of investment fraud**, with total losses of **€20 million**. These are only the cases reported to the FMA.

## Fraudsters have become more professional

The FMA explains that investment fraud is often harder to recognise than a few years ago. Fraudsters use **social media**, **messenger services** such as WhatsApp or Telegram, and **AI-generated images, videos and voices** to create credible identities and serious-looking offers. Victims are often invited into **chat groups** where supposed investors report high profits and self-proclaimed experts give recommendations.

Investor.gov describes the same pattern: scammers use fake testimonials, pay people to post fake reviews, and use altered images or AI-generated videos to make it look as if others are getting rich.

## Why it works

Fraud exploits the same biases explained in Category O:

- **Herding and FOMO:** a chat group full of "winners" creates pressure to join in (Category O).
- **Trust and authority:** fake experts, fake regulators and cloned company names borrow credibility (lesson 4).
- **Greed and hope:** promises of high, safe returns are hard to resist, especially in difficult times.
- **Commitment:** after a first "profit", victims send more, and later pay "fees" to get their money back (lesson 7).
- **Time pressure:** urgency stops people from checking (lesson 2).

> [!NOTE] Anyone can be a victim
> Fraudsters target people of every age, education level and income. Being careful is not about intelligence; it is about following a few fixed checks every time (lesson 3).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - The FMA received 843 investment fraud reports in 2025, with €20 million in losses.
> - Fraudsters use social media, messenger chat groups and AI-generated content to look credible.
> - They exploit herding, trust, greed, commitment and time pressure.
> - Anyone can be targeted; fixed checks protect better than confidence.
