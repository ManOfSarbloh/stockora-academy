Fraudulent offers come in many forms, but they share recognisable patterns. If you see **one** of these signs, stop and check. If you see **several**, walk away.

## The FMA's warning signs (2026)

The FMA lists these as particular reasons for caution:

1. **Exceptionally high or seemingly risk-free returns** are promised.
2. **Time pressure** is applied: "only today", "last places".
3. Communication happens **only through messenger services**.
4. You are asked to **install remote access software** on your computer or phone.
5. You are asked to **share personal access data** such as passwords or login codes.

The FMA also advises: invest only in products you **understand**, check the **provider** carefully, and look for **warnings from supervisory authorities** about the company (lesson 3).

> [!WARNING] Never install remote access software for an "investment adviser"
> Remote access lets a stranger control your computer and, potentially, your online banking. No legitimate investment firm needs it.

## Unsolicited contact

The FMA warned in 2018 never to conclude financial transactions if a provider contacted you **unsolicited** by phone, fax or email, describing such "cold calling" as **prohibited by law in Austria** and a sure warning sign of fraudulent intentions.

## The SEC's red flags for Ponzi schemes

Investor.gov lists warning signs that many frauds share:

- **High returns with little or no risk.** Every investment carries risk; be highly suspicious of anything "guaranteed".
- **Overly consistent returns.** Real investments go up and down; steady gains regardless of markets are suspicious.
- **Unregistered investments** and **unlicensed sellers.**
- Secretive or overly complex strategies, missing paperwork, and difficulty getting your money out.

## Signs from relationship and crypto scams

Investor.gov describes further patterns (lesson 6):

- You are asked to send money as **crypto assets**, for example via a **bitcoin ATM**, or by wire transfer.
- Your account shows impressive **profits you cannot withdraw**; instead you are told to pay more "fees" or "taxes" first (lesson 7).
- **Fake testimonials**, paid reviews and AI-generated videos suggest others are getting rich.

> [!EXAMPLE] A typical message
> "Hi, sorry, wrong number! ... By the way, I'm a trader with a 2% daily return using an AI strategy. I can add you to our VIP group. Spots are limited." Wrong-number contact, unrealistic return, a group, artificial scarcity: four red flags in three sentences.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - High or risk-free returns, time pressure and messenger-only contact are classic warning signs (FMA).
> - Never install remote access software or share login details at a stranger's request.
> - The FMA has described unsolicited cold calling for financial transactions as illegal in Austria.
> - Overly consistent returns and unlicensed sellers are typical of Ponzi schemes (SEC).
> - One red flag: stop and check. Several: walk away.
