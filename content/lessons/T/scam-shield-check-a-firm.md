Before you send money to any firm, run these checks. They are free, take about ten minutes, and catch most fraudulent offers.

> [!NOTE] Scam Shield: the five checks
> 1. **Is it authorised?** Search the FMA's **company database** (*Unternehmensdatenbank*).
> 2. **Is there a warning?** Search the FMA's **warning list**.
> 3. **Is there an international alert?** Search IOSCO's **I-SCAN**.
> 4. **Do the contact details match?** Compare website, email, phone and address with the official register, not with what the firm sent you.
> 5. **Still unsure?** Contact the regulator directly, using contact details from the regulator's own website.

## Check 1: the FMA company database

Every FMA warning ends with the same reminder: anyone who offers banking, financial or investment services in Austria needs **permission from the FMA**, and some companies offer such services without it. Whether a company is authorised can be checked in the **FMA's company database**. Check also **which services** it is authorised for: a firm registered for one activity may not be allowed to offer another.

## Check 2: the FMA warning list

The FMA publishes **warnings** about providers that are not authorised to offer their services in Austria. Each warning names the provider and the web address and contact details it uses, and states which regulated activities it is **not** allowed to carry out. New warnings appear every week. A warning means: do not do business with that provider.

> [!TIP] The FMA app
> The FMA offers a free app with access to its company database and immediate notification of new warnings.

## Check 3: international alerts on I-SCAN

The **International Securities & Commodities Alerts Network (I-SCAN)**, run by IOSCO, the international organisation of securities regulators, collects warnings submitted by regulators around the world and makes them searchable. It contains tens of thousands of entries. IOSCO notes that it facilitates access to members' information but does not independently verify all of it, so treat it as one source among several.

## Check 4: verify contact details independently

The FMA warns that fraudsters **clone** the identity of genuine, registered firms and refer to the real firm's entry in official databases. Others invent a **regulator** that does not exist, complete with a fake register (lesson 4). So:

- Compare the website, email domain, phone number and address with those in the **official** register.
- Check that the regulator named actually **exists** and is **responsible**.
- Contact the firm using the details from the register, not from the offer.

## Check 5: ask the regulator

The FMA encourages people not to hesitate to contact the relevant authority directly, independently of the provider and its website.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Anyone offering banking or investment services in Austria needs FMA permission; check the FMA company database.
> - Search the FMA warning list and IOSCO's international I-SCAN alerts.
> - Compare all contact details with the official register; clone firms copy real firms' data.
> - Contact regulators directly, using details from their own websites.
> - The checks take minutes; skipping them can cost your savings.
