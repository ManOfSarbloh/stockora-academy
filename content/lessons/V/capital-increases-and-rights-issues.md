## Why companies raise capital

A **capital increase** brings new equity into a company by increasing its share capital (Wiener Börse). Companies do it to finance growth or acquisitions, to reduce debt, or to strengthen their balance sheet after losses (Category I). Two main forms:

- **For cash:** new shares are offered to existing shareholders through **subscription rights** in proportion to their holdings.
- **For contributions in kind:** someone brings in an asset, such as property or another company, and receives new shares for it.

## The rules in Austria

> [!AUSTRIA] What the Stock Corporation Act requires
> - A capital increase by issuing new shares needs a majority of **at least three quarters** of the share capital represented at the vote (§ 149 AktG).
> - Every shareholder is entitled, on request, to a part of the new shares matching their **existing share** of the capital (§ 153 AktG).
> - The period for exercising the subscription right must be **at least two weeks**; the issue price and the period must be published.
> - The subscription right can only be **excluded** in the resolution on the increase, with a three-quarters majority, if announced in the agenda, and with a written report from the management board explaining the reason and the issue price.

## The arithmetic

> [!EXAMPLE] Alpenrad AG rights issue (fictional)
> - 10 million shares at **€20** before the issue: market value €200 million.
> - Ratio **1 for 4**: for every 4 shares held, you may buy 1 new share at **€16**.
> - New shares: 2.5 million, raising €40 million.
>
> **Theoretical price after the issue** (theoretical ex-rights price):
> (€200 million + €40 million) ÷ 12.5 million shares = **€19.20**.
>
> **Theoretical value of one right** (one right per old share):
> €20 − €19.20 = **€0.80**. Check: 4 rights (€3.20) + €16 = €19.20, the price of one new share.

## Your three options

With 400 Alpenrad shares (€8,000 before the issue), you receive 400 rights:

| Option | What you do | Result (theoretical) |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscribe** | Pay 100 × €16 = €1,600 | 500 shares × €19.20 = €9,600 (€8,000 + €1,600 paid in); your **percentage stays the same** |
| **Sell the rights** | Sell 400 rights at about €0.80 | 400 shares × €19.20 = €7,680 + €320 cash = €8,000; your percentage falls |
| **Do nothing** | The rights may expire, or your bank may sell them depending on its terms | 400 shares × €19.20 = **€7,680**; you **lose €320** and your percentage falls |

> [!WARNING] Doing nothing costs money
> The share price falls after the issue because new shares are sold below the old price. The rights compensate for that only if you use or sell them. Read your bank's notice and its deadline.

## Capital cuts in a crisis

A company in trouble may first **reduce** its share capital to absorb losses and then increase it again, often to the original amount: a **capital cut** (*Kapitalschnitt*). In a restructuring, shareholders are asked to put in new money (Wiener Börse). If existing shareholders don't take part, their stake can shrink dramatically.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A capital increase for cash usually gives existing shareholders subscription rights.
> - In Austria, the subscription period is at least two weeks; excluding the right requires a three-quarters majority and a report.
> - New shares are typically priced below the market, so the share price falls after the issue.
> - Subscribing keeps your percentage; selling the rights keeps your value; doing nothing loses value.
> - A capital cut in a crisis can dilute shareholders who don't put in new money.
