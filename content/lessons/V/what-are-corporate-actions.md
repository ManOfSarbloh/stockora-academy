A **corporate action** is any event decided by a company that changes something about its shares: how many there are, what they are worth per share, or what you receive. Owning shares means you will meet them sooner or later.

## Three kinds

| Kind | What happens | Examples | Your role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Automatic** | Applied to all shareholders | Cash dividend, stock split, reverse split, bonus shares | Nothing to do; check that your account is updated |
| **With a choice** | You can pick between options | Rights issue (subscribe, sell the rights, or let them lapse) | Decide by a deadline, or a default applies |
| **Voluntary** | You decide whether to take part | Takeover offer, issuer tender offer (the company offers to buy back its own shares) | Accept, or keep your shares |

## How you hear about them

- Companies publish corporate actions in official announcements (Category R).
- Your **bank or broker** usually informs you and asks for your instruction when you have a choice. Its deadline can be **earlier** than the company's, because it needs time to pass on your instruction.
- If you do nothing, a **default** applies. For subscription rights, the law requires a subscription period of **at least two weeks** (§ 153 AktG), but if you neither exercise nor sell them, they can expire.

> [!WARNING] Orders can be deleted
> The Wiener Börse explains that a security can be suspended from trading, for example when important company news or a takeover offer could affect its price, or during capital measures. A suspension means **all orders in the order book are automatically deleted**. Check your open limit and stop orders after a suspension (Category F).

## What changes, and what doesn't

The key question for every corporate action: **does the value of my holding change, or only how it is divided up?**

- A **split** or **bonus shares** only divide the same value into more pieces (lesson 2).
- A **dividend** moves value from the company to you; the share price usually falls by about the dividend on the ex-date (Category G).
- A **capital increase** brings new money in and new shares out; if you don't take part, your percentage falls (lesson 3).
- A **takeover offer** usually comes at a premium to the market price, which Investor.gov notes is meant to encourage shareholders to sell (lesson 5).

> [!EXAMPLE] A typical year at Alpenrad AG (fictional)
> - April: annual general meeting (lesson 8) approves a dividend.
> - May: ex-date and dividend payment (Category G).
> - September: 2-for-1 share split; your 100 shares become 200.
> - November: rights issue; your bank asks whether you want to subscribe.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Corporate actions change the number, price or value of shares.
> - Some are automatic; others need your decision by a deadline.
> - Your bank's deadline may be earlier than the company's; a default applies if you do nothing.
> - A trading suspension deletes all orders in the order book.
> - Always ask: does the value change, or only how it is divided?
