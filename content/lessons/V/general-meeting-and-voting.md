## What the general meeting is

The Wiener Börse describes the **general meeting** (*Hauptversammlung*) as the body through which shareholders exercise their rights in company matters. It decides on important steps and usually meets **once a year**. Typical agenda items:

- using the profit, including the **dividend** (Category G);
- **discharging** the management and supervisory boards;
- **electing** supervisory board members and the auditor (Category R);
- **capital measures** such as capital increases (lesson 3) and buyback authorisations (lesson 4);
- changes to the articles of association.

## Your rights

| Right | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Voting right** | Voting for or against proposals. One share usually carries one vote; non-voting preference shares also exist (Wiener Börse; Category C). |
| **Right to information** | Each shareholder can ask for information about company matters, as far as it is needed to assess an agenda item (§ 118 AktG). |
| **Right to propose** | Shareholders can put their own motions at the meeting (Wiener Börse). |

> [!NOTE] Limits to the right to information
> Under § 118 AktG, the company may refuse information that, on reasonable business judgement, could seriously harm the company, or whose disclosure would be a criminal offence. It may also refer to answers that were published on its website as questions and answers at least seven days before the meeting.

## Key dates in Austria

> [!AUSTRIA] Notice and record date
> - **Notice:** an annual general meeting must be called at least **28 days** in advance; other meetings at least **21 days** (§ 107 AktG). Listed companies must publish the notice in a way that reaches investors across the EU quickly.
> - **Record date** (*Nachweisstichtag*): for bearer shares of listed companies, what counts is your holding at the **end of the tenth day before the meeting** (§ 111 AktG). Shares bought after that day don't give you the right to attend; shares sold after it don't take it away.
> - **Deposit confirmation:** for shares held in a securities account, you prove your holding with a confirmation from your bank, which must reach the company by the **third working day** before the meeting, unless the notice sets a later time.

> [!EXAMPLE] Taking part in Alpenrad AG's meeting (fictional)
> - 20 March: notice published (meeting on 24 April, 35 days later).
> - 14 April, end of day: record date (tenth day before 24 April).
> - Ask your bank for a deposit confirmation; it must reach the company by the third working day before the meeting.
> - 24 April: attend in person, or use the voting options offered in the notice (for example a proxy).

## Is it worth it for a small shareholder?

One small shareholder rarely changes a vote. But the meeting is a chance to hear management, read the documents and ask questions, and every vote counts in the result. The notice and documents, published in advance, are useful reading even if you don't attend (Category W).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - The general meeting decides on dividends, board elections, auditors and capital measures.
> - Shareholders have rights to vote, to information and to make proposals.
> - In Austria, the AGM must be called at least 28 days ahead.
> - Your holding at the end of the tenth day before the meeting decides who may take part.
> - A bank deposit confirmation must reach the company by the third working day before the meeting.
