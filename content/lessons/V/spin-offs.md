## What a spin-off is

Investor.gov describes a **spin-off** as a parent company **distributing shares of a subsidiary** to its own shareholders, so that the subsidiary becomes a **separate, independent company**. The shares are usually distributed **pro rata**, in proportion to your holding. Whether shareholders must approve depends on company law and exchange rules.

Investor.gov also points out that the SEC does **not evaluate the merits** of a spin-off or whether the shares are good investments; information is in the disclosure documents.

## Why companies do it

Common reasons given by companies include focusing each business on its own strategy, giving investors a clearer view of two different businesses, or preparing a part for a separate future. Whether a spin-off creates value is a question for each case.

## What happens to your holding

> [!EXAMPLE] Alpenrad AG spins off its e-bike unit (fictional)
> - Before: you own 100 Alpenrad shares at €30 = **€3,000**.
> - Ratio: **1 new "Voltrad" share for every 4 Alpenrad shares**. You receive 25 Voltrad shares.
> - After the spin-off, Alpenrad trades at €24 and Voltrad at €24.
> - Your holding: 100 × €24 + 25 × €24 = **€3,000**.
>
> The value was split, not created. From now on, the two shares move independently.

## Practical points

- **Fractional entitlements:** with 102 shares at a 1-for-4 ratio, you would be entitled to 25.5 new shares. How fractions are handled (cash payment, rounding, sale) depends on the terms; check your bank's notice.
- **Price drop on the ex-date:** the parent's share price falls because part of the company is no longer included. This is not a loss.
- **Your cost basis:** for tax, the original purchase cost may need to be split between the two holdings. The rules are technical and depend on how the spin-off is structured; ask your bank or check with the tax office (Category Q).
- **Index changes:** the new company may or may not be included in the same indexes as the parent, which can lead to buying or selling by index funds (Category H).
- **Order book:** trading can be suspended around such capital measures, and a suspension deletes open orders (Wiener Börse; lesson 1).

> [!MISTAKE] Selling the "small leftover" without looking
> Many investors sell spin-off shares simply because they never chose to buy them. Treat them like any other investment decision: look at the business, its finances and its valuation (Category I).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A spin-off distributes a subsidiary's shares to the parent's shareholders, usually pro rata.
> - On the day, total value stays the same; afterwards the two shares move independently.
> - Fractions, cost basis and tax treatment depend on the terms and the rules; check with your bank.
> - The SEC does not judge whether a spin-off is a good investment.
> - Decide about spin-off shares as you would about any investment.
