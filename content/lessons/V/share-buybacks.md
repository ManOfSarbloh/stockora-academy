## What a buyback is

In a **share buyback**, a company uses its own money to buy its shares, usually on the stock exchange, sometimes through an offer to all shareholders (an *issuer tender offer*, Investor.gov). The shares bought back are then held as **own shares** (treasury shares) or **cancelled**.

> [!AUSTRIA] The rules in Austria
> The Wiener Börse notes that a buyback requires an authorisation by the **general meeting**, and its purposes are bound by company law. Under § 65 AktG, for a listed company:
> - The authorisation may be valid for **at most 30 months**. It must state the share of capital to be acquired and the lowest and highest price.
> - Own shares may not exceed **10% of the share capital** in total.
> - **Trading** in own shares is not allowed as a purpose.
> - The resolution and, just before it starts, the buyback programme and its duration must be **published**.
> - The company has **no rights** from its own shares: no votes, no dividends.

## What it does to the numbers

> [!EXAMPLE] Alpenrad AG buys back 5% (fictional)
> - Profit: €50 million; shares: 10 million; earnings per share (EPS): **€5.00**.
> - The company spends €50 million to buy back 500,000 shares at €100 and cancels them.
> - New EPS, with the same profit: €50 million ÷ 9.5 million = **€5.26**, about **5.3% higher**.
>
> But the company now has €50 million less cash. If that cash had earned interest, profit would fall slightly. A higher EPS does not automatically mean a more valuable company.

## Buyback or dividend?

Both return money to shareholders:

| | Dividend | Buyback |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets cash | Every shareholder, in proportion | Only those who sell |
| If you don't sell | You receive cash (taxed, Category Q) | You own a slightly larger percentage |
| Shares outstanding | Unchanged | Fall, if the shares are cancelled |

## Judging a buyback

Questions worth asking:

- **Price:** buying back shares is good for remaining shareholders only if the price is **below** what the shares are worth (Category I). Buying expensive shares destroys value.
- **Source of money:** paid from spare cash, or from **borrowing**? Debt-financed buybacks raise financial risk (Category I).
- **Offsetting dilution:** some buybacks mainly offset new shares issued to employees and managers (§ 65 AktG also allows buybacks for employee share plans). The share count may not fall at all.
- **Announcement vs reality:** an authorisation allows a buyback; it does not oblige the company to buy. Check later reports for what was actually bought.

> [!MISTAKE] "Buybacks always push the price up"
> A buyback adds demand for the shares while it runs, but the price still depends on the business and on what the market expects. A company can buy back shares and still see its price fall.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A buyback reduces the number of shares, so each remaining share owns more of the company.
> - In Austria: authorisation by the general meeting, valid for up to 30 months, capped at 10% of share capital, published.
> - Own shares carry no votes and no dividends.
> - EPS rises after a buyback even if profit doesn't; that alone doesn't create value.
> - Judge the price paid, the source of money, and whether shares are actually cancelled.
