Risk management does not require complex models. Most damage comes from a few avoidable mistakes: too much in one place, borrowed money, products that are not understood, and money needed too soon.

## Before buying anything

> [!NOTE] Twelve questions
> 1. **Do I understand it?** Can I explain in two sentences how it makes or loses money? (Investor.gov: never invest in something you don't understand.)
> 2. **Is the seller authorised?** (Category T)
> 3. **What is the worst realistic loss?** Could it go to zero? Could I lose more than I put in?
> 4. **What does the KID's risk indicator (1–7) say?** It combines market and issuer credit risk (lesson 2).
> 5. **Is there leverage?** If so, how much? (Category N)
> 6. **Who else must pay me?** Issuer, counterparty, broker? (lesson 1)
> 7. **Can I sell it quickly at a fair price?** Liquidity risk (lesson 1).
> 8. **What currency is it in?** (Category L)
> 9. **What does it cost in total?** (Category G)
> 10. **How big is this position in my portfolio?** (lesson 6)
> 11. **When might I need this money?** (Category M)
> 12. **Why am I buying it now?** If the answer is "everyone is talking about it", wait (Category O).

## Once a year: the portfolio review

- **Allocation:** is the mix of shares, bonds and cash still on target? Rebalance if needed (Category M).
- **Concentration:** does any company, sector or country exceed your limits?
- **Overlap:** do several funds hold the same companies?
- **Drawdown test:** what would a fall like 2008 (−36.6% for US shares) mean in euros for you? Could you live with it? (lesson 2, Category M)
- **Life changes:** new job, children, home purchase, approaching retirement?
- **Safety net:** is the emergency fund still adequate?
- **Where your assets are held:** with authorised institutions? In Austria, securities in a custody account remain your property; investor compensation covers up to €20,000 if they cannot be delivered (Einlagensicherung AUSTRIA).

## Warning signs

> [!WARNING] Stop and reconsider if…
> - you are borrowing to invest, or planning to;
> - one investment would hurt your life plans if it went to zero;
> - you cannot explain the product;
> - you are checking prices several times a day and losing sleep;
> - someone promises high returns with little or no risk. Investor.gov notes that many investment frauds are pitched exactly this way.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Most investment damage comes from concentration, leverage, misunderstood products and money needed too soon.
> - Run through a checklist before every purchase.
> - Review allocation, concentration, overlap and drawdown tolerance once a year.
> - Promises of high returns with little risk are a classic fraud signal.
