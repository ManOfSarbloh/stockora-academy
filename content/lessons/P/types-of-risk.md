Investor.gov defines **risk** in finance as the degree of **uncertainty and/or potential financial loss** inherent in an investment decision. In general, as risks rise, investors seek higher returns to compensate. All investments involve some risk, including "safe" ones.

## The main types

| Risk | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Business risk** | The company does badly or fails. | Shareholders are last in line if a company is liquidated (Investor.gov). |
| **Volatility (market) risk** | Prices swing, even if the company is fine. | Investor.gov notes that large company shares as a group have lost money on average about one year in three. |
| **Inflation risk** | Rising prices erode purchasing power. | The main concern for money held in cash (Investor.gov). |
| **Interest rate risk** | Rate changes move the value of bonds, and also shares (Category L). | A bond sold before maturity may be worth less than its face value. |
| **Liquidity risk** | You cannot sell when you want, or only at a poor price. | More complicated products, small companies, products with exit penalties. |
| **Currency risk** | Exchange rates change the value of foreign investments (Category L). | US shares bought in euros. |
| **Counterparty and issuer risk** | The other party to a contract, or the issuer of a certificate, cannot pay (Category N). | A bank that issued a certificate goes bankrupt. |
| **Concentration risk** | Too much depends on one company, sector or country (Category M). | Most of your savings in your employer's shares. |

## Which risks can be reduced?

- **Specific risk** affects one company or sector: a failed product, a fraud, a lost lawsuit. It can be reduced a lot by **diversifying** across many companies (Category M).
- **Systematic (market) risk** affects almost everything at once: recessions, interest rate shocks, pandemics. Diversification across shares cannot remove it. It can only be reduced by holding less in risky assets, or by mixing asset classes that behave differently (lesson 4).

> [!EXAMPLE] Two kinds of bad news
> - A single pharmaceutical company's key drug fails: its shares might fall 50%. In a portfolio of 500 companies, the effect is tiny.
> - A deep recession: most shares fall together. A portfolio of 500 shares falls too.

## Risks of holding your investments

Your account itself carries some risks:

> [!AUSTRIA] What happens if your bank or broker fails?
> Einlagensicherung AUSTRIA explains that securities held in a customer's custody account are the **customer's property**, held only in safe custody by the bank, and must be transferred on request. They are therefore generally **not** a case for deposit protection. Only if, in a compensation case, the bank cannot transfer or deliver the securities, the **investor compensation** scheme secures claims **up to €20,000**. Category F explains the details.

## Risk is not the enemy

Taking no risk also has a cost: the SEC's guide notes that cash is safest, but inflation can erode its value, and that long-term goals usually require some investments with higher risk. Risk management is not about avoiding all risk; it is about taking **the right risks, in the right amount**, for your goals.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Risk means uncertainty and possible loss; higher risk usually comes with higher expected return.
> - Main types: business, market, inflation, interest rate, liquidity, currency, counterparty and concentration risk.
> - Company-specific risk can be diversified away; market-wide risk cannot.
> - Securities in a custody account belong to you; investor compensation in Austria covers up to €20,000 if they cannot be delivered.
> - Avoiding all risk has its own cost, especially through inflation.
