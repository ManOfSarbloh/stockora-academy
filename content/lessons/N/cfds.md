A **contract for difference (CFD)** is an agreement between you and a provider to exchange the difference in the price of an underlying, such as a share, an index, a currency pair or gold, between the opening and closing of the contract. You never own the underlying. CFDs are usually traded with **leverage**, so a small deposit controls a large position (lesson 3).

## Why regulators stepped in

In 2018, ESMA and the national regulators concluded that CFDs raised a **significant investor protection concern** because of their complexity, lack of transparency, **excessive leverage** and the way they were marketed. National analyses showed that **74–89% of retail accounts typically lose money**, with average losses per client of **€1,600 to €29,000** (ESMA). France's AMF had earlier found that more than 89% of active forex and CFD clients lost money over four years (lesson 2).

ESMA's chair said the combination of the promise of high returns, easy-to-use digital platforms and historically low interest rates had created an offer that appealed to retail investors, but that the products' complexity and excessive leverage had resulted in significant losses.

## The rules for retail clients

ESMA's measures, applied from **1 August 2018**, consisted of five elements:

1. **Leverage limits** when opening a position, depending on the underlying:

| Underlying | Maximum leverage | Minimum margin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Major currency pairs | 30:1 | 3.33% |
| Other currency pairs, gold, major indices | 20:1 | 5% |
| Commodities other than gold, other indices | 10:1 | 10% |
| Individual shares and other underlyings | 5:1 | 20% |
| Cryptocurrencies | 2:1 | 50% |

2. A **margin close-out rule**: the provider must close positions when the client's funds fall to **50%** of the minimum required margin.
3. **Negative balance protection**: a retail client cannot lose more than the money in the CFD account.
4. A ban on **incentives** such as bonuses for trading.
5. A standardised **risk warning** stating the percentage of the provider's retail accounts that lose money.

ESMA also **banned** the sale of **binary options** to retail investors, citing a structural expected negative return and an embedded conflict of interest between providers and clients.

> [!AUSTRIA] Permanent rules in Austria
> ESMA's measures could only be imposed for three months at a time. To keep the protection in place, the FMA issued the **FMA Product Intervention Regulation (FMA-PIV)**, in force since **15 May 2019**. It **prohibits** marketing, distributing or selling binary options to private clients and **severely restricts** CFDs with leverage limits, a close-out rule, negative balance protection, a ban on incentives and clear risk warnings. The consolidated text in the RIS lists the same minimum margins as the table above.

## What the rules do and do not do

- They **limit** how much you can lose: not more than your deposit, with automatic close-outs.
- They do **not** make CFDs profitable. Most retail accounts still lose money, which is why providers must show the loss percentage.
- They protect **retail (private) clients**. Clients classified as professional do not benefit from these limits.

> [!WARNING] Read the risk warning
> Every CFD provider in the EU must state what percentage of its retail accounts lose money. Take that number seriously: it describes what actually happened to customers like you.

> [!MISTAKE] "CFDs are like shares, just cheaper"
> A CFD is a contract with a provider, not ownership of the share. You have no shareholder rights such as voting, positions held overnight can carry financing costs, and the prices you trade at come from the provider.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A CFD is a leveraged contract on the price difference of an underlying; you never own it.
> - National regulators found 74–89% of retail CFD accounts typically lose money (ESMA, 2018).
> - EU retail rules: leverage limits from 30:1 to 2:1, close-out at 50% of margin, negative balance protection, no incentives, standard risk warning.
> - Austria's FMA-PIV has made these rules permanent since 15 May 2019 and bans binary options for private clients.
> - The rules cap losses; they do not make CFD trading profitable.
