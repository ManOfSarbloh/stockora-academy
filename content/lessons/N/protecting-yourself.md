The evidence in this category is clear: most retail traders lose money (lesson 2). If you decide to try anyway, perhaps to learn or out of curiosity, these rules limit the damage.

## 1. Separate it from your real investments

Keep trading money in a **separate account** with a **fixed budget** you can afford to lose completely, such as a small percentage of your savings. Your long-term plan (Category M) stays untouched. Never top up the trading budget after losses.

## 2. Use only regulated providers

Check that the provider is authorised in the EU, for example in the FMA's company database (Category T). Under the EU and Austrian rules, CFD providers for retail clients must offer **negative balance protection**, close positions at **50%** of the required margin, and show the share of their retail accounts that **lose money** (lesson 7). An offer of very high leverage to a private client in Austria is a warning sign.

> [!WARNING] Unregulated offers
> Firms that promise guaranteed profits, bonuses for trading or "signals" that cannot lose are often unauthorised or fraudulent. The FMA publishes warnings about such firms (Category T).

## 3. No borrowed money, no leverage at first

Leverage turns small mistakes into large losses (lesson 3). If you want to learn how markets move, start without leverage, with real shares in small amounts.

## 4. Practise without money first

Many platforms offer **demo or paper trading** accounts (Category J). They cannot reproduce the emotions of real losses, but they let you learn the mechanics without paying for mistakes.

## 5. Write your rules before you trade

- Maximum amount per trade (for example 1–2% of the trading budget at risk, Category P).
- Where you will exit if the trade goes wrong.
- A **stop for the whole experiment**: "If the budget falls by half, I stop."

## 6. Keep an honest record

Write down every trade with costs. After a few months, compare your result with simply having bought a broad index fund. The AMF found that the clients who traded the most **lost the most**, and that there was **no learning effect over time**. An honest record protects you from remembering only the wins.

## 7. Be wary of courses, signals and "challenges"

Courses, paid signal groups and prop trading challenges earn money from traders whether or not the traders make money. The FMA notes that paid challenges can resemble a casino in which the company sets the rules and lives mainly from participation fees (lesson 1).

> [!TIP] Use the SEC's five questions
> Is the seller licensed? Is the product registered or documented? How do risks compare with rewards? Do you understand it? Where can you get independent help? (Investor.gov)

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Keep trading money separate, small and fixed; never chase losses.
> - Use only regulated providers; EU rules give retail CFD clients negative balance protection.
> - Avoid borrowed money and leverage, and practise on a demo account first.
> - Set written rules, including when to stop altogether.
> - Track every trade honestly and compare with a simple index investment.
