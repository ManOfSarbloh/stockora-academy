Advertising for trading apps and courses shows successful traders. The data collected by regulators tells a different story.

## France: nine out of ten lose (AMF, 2014)

The French market regulator **AMF** asked authorised providers of forex and CFD trading for their clients' actual results. Over a **four-year** observation period, covering **14,799 active clients**, it found:

- **More than 89%** of clients lost money.
- The **average** result was a loss of **€10,887** per client; the **median** result was a loss of **€1,843**.
- In total, clients lost about **€161 million**.
- In any single year, the share of losing clients was between **75% and 89%**, depending on the provider and year, and close to 84% overall.

The AMF concluded that the study confirmed the extremely high risk of these forms of trading, which are unsuitable for the vast majority of private investors.

## The EU: 74–89% of accounts lose (ESMA, 2018)

When the European Securities and Markets Authority restricted CFDs in 2018, it cited analyses by national regulators across the EU: **74–89% of retail accounts typically lose money** on CFDs, with average losses per client between **€1,600 and €29,000**. That is why CFD providers in the EU must state the percentage of their retail accounts that lose money in a standard risk warning (lesson 7).

## Taiwan: exchange data on every day trade

A study by Brad Barber, Yi-Tsung Lee, Yu-Jane Liu and Terrance Odean (working paper, 2004) used data from the **Taiwan Stock Exchange**, where day trading by individuals made up more than 20% of total volume in 1995–1999. The authors found:

- In a typical six-month period, **more than eight out of ten day traders lost money**.
- **Heavy day traders** earned profits before costs, but **not enough to cover transaction costs**.
- A **relatively small group** of traders showed persistent skill.

## Why the results are so poor

- **Costs:** spreads, fees and financing costs are paid on every trade, whether it wins or loses (lesson 1).
- **Leverage:** small price moves become large gains or losses (lesson 3).
- **Competition and conflicts:** many trades are against professionals, and with some products the provider's interests can run against the client's; ESMA pointed to an embedded conflict of interest between binary option providers and their clients.
- **Psychology:** overconfidence, chasing losses and overtrading (Category O).

> [!WARNING] "But I will be in the winning minority"
> Almost everyone who starts trading believes this. The studies above counted all of those people. Even the Taiwan study's skilled minority was small, and nobody can know in advance whether they belong to it.

> [!MISTAKE] Judging trading by a few good weeks
> Short winning streaks happen by chance, even when the expected result is negative. The AMF's figures cover four years of real accounts; a few weeks of your own results say very little.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - AMF (France): more than 89% of 14,799 active forex and CFD clients lost money over four years; average loss €10,887.
> - ESMA: national regulators found 74–89% of retail CFD accounts typically lose money.
> - Taiwan exchange data: in a typical six-month period, more than eight in ten day traders lost money.
> - Costs, leverage, professional competition and psychology explain much of the result.
> - Investor.gov calls day trading extremely risky.
