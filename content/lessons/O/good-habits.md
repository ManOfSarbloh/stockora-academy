Knowing about biases is a start, but it does not make you immune (lesson 1). A more reliable strategy is to **design your environment** so that good decisions happen automatically.

## Plans versus temptations

The Nobel committee describes Richard Thaler's **planner-doer** model of self-control: an internal tension between long-term planning and short-term doing. Giving in to short-term temptation is an important reason why plans to save for old age often fail. Thaler showed how **nudging**, a term he coined, can help people exercise better self-control, for example when saving for a pension.

You can nudge yourself.

## Seven habits

**1. Automate saving and investing.** A monthly savings plan (Category M) invests without a decision each month. Investor.gov notes that investing equal amounts at regular intervals means buying more when prices are low.

**2. Write it down.** A written investment plan (Category M) says what you will do and why. Decide in calm times what you will do in a crash.

**3. Use a checklist before every purchase.** For example: What is it? What does it cost? What are the main risks? Why do I want it now? Would I buy it if nobody else was talking about it? The FMA recommends critically questioning why you prefer a particular investment.

**4. Look less often.** Checking prices daily makes every fluctuation feel important and tempts you to act. A quarterly or yearly review is enough for a long-term portfolio.

**5. Add friction to trading.** A rule such as "wait 48 hours before any trade that is not in my plan" gives the planning side time to catch up with the doing side.

**6. Keep a decision diary.** Record why you bought or sold. It protects against hindsight ("I knew it all along") and self-attribution (lesson 3).

**7. Compare with a simple benchmark.** Barber and Odean's data showed the most active traders earning far less than the market (lesson 1). Compare your results honestly with a broad index fund.

> [!EXAMPLE] A personal rulebook
> - I invest €200 on the 5th of every month into my chosen funds.
> - I look at my portfolio once a quarter.
> - I rebalance once a year if a category is more than 5 points off target.
> - I make no trade outside the plan without waiting 48 hours and writing down the reason.
> - When markets fall 20%, I re-read this page instead of my news feed.

## When things go wrong

Everyone makes mistakes. The aim is not to be perfect but to keep mistakes **small and rare**: diversify (Category M), avoid leverage (Category N), and never put money you need soon into shares.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Awareness of biases is not enough; design your environment so good choices are automatic.
> - Thaler's work shows how nudges help people stick to long-term plans.
> - Automate investing, write down a plan and use a checklist before each purchase.
> - Look at prices less often and add a waiting period before unplanned trades.
> - Keep a decision diary and compare your results with a simple benchmark.
