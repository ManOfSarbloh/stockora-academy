## What herding is

The FMA describes **herd behaviour**: under the conditions of crowd psychology, investors often do not decide rationally but imitate the behaviour of the majority. Especially in times of uncertainty, such as shocks on financial markets, the number of people who focus on facts often falls. Because many people act in the same direction at the same time, prices swing strongly as investors pile into an investment, or out of it.

## FOMO: the fear of missing out

**FOMO** is the feeling that everyone else is getting rich and you are being left behind. It is herding with an emotional engine:

- A share or asset has risen sharply.
- Friends, colleagues and social media talk about their gains.
- You buy, often near the top, because not buying feels more painful than the risk of losing.

> [!EXAMPLE] A pattern from 2021
> In January 2021, GameStop shares rose more than 1,600% in about two weeks amid enormous attention on Reddit and in the media (SEC staff report). Investors who bought late in January, when the excitement was greatest, paid more than $300 per share; within three weeks the price had fallen to around $40 (Category N).

## Herding in panics

Herding works on the way down too. Federal Reserve History describes how, on Black Monday 1987, sell orders vastly outnumbered buyers and the selling cascaded from market to market; Intel's CEO compared it to a theatre where someone yells "Fire!". In 1929, investors "began selling madly" after a failed attempt to restore confidence (Category K).

## Why we follow the crowd

- **Social proof:** if many people do something, we assume they know something.
- **Fear of standing out:** being wrong alone feels worse than being wrong together.
- **Information cascades:** each buyer sees others buying and treats that as evidence, even if nobody has new information.

## How to resist

The FMA suggests that you critically question **why** you currently prefer a particular investment and why others seem unattractive. Becoming aware of these influences can lead to better investing. Practical steps:

- **Write down your reason** for a purchase before you make it. "Everyone is buying" is not a reason.
- **Wait 48 hours** before acting on a hot tip.
- **Check valuations** (Category I). Rising prices are not evidence of value.
- **Stick to your plan** (Category M). A savings plan into a broad fund does not need to chase themes.

> [!MISTAKE] "The price keeps going up, so they must be right"
> Rising prices can be a sign that many people are buying for the same reason, which is exactly how bubbles grow (Category K).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Investors often imitate the majority, especially under uncertainty (FMA).
> - Herding amplifies price swings in both directions: bubbles and panics.
> - FOMO pushes people to buy late, near peaks.
> - Ask yourself why you prefer an investment, and write the reason down.
> - A written plan and a waiting period help resist the crowd.
