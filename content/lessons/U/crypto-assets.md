## The regulators' warning

In March 2022, the three European Supervisory Authorities (**EBA, ESMA and EIOPA**) warned consumers that **many crypto-assets are highly risky and speculative**. They said these assets are not suited for most retail consumers as an investment or as a means of payment, because consumers:

- face the **very real possibility of losing all their invested money**;
- should be alert to **misleading advertisements**, including via social media and influencers;
- should be particularly wary of promised **fast or high returns** that look too good to be true.

## Three kinds of crypto-assets under MiCAR

The EU's **Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCAR)** sets common rules across the EU. The FMA explains three categories:

| Category | What it is | Example (FMA) |
|---|---|---|
| **Asset-referenced tokens (ARTs)** | Value linked to one or more assets, such as currencies, commodities like gold, or other tokens | A token worth a basket of euro, US dollar and gold |
| **E-money tokens (EMTs)** | Linked 1:1 to a single official currency, intended for payments | A token always worth exactly €1 |
| **Other crypto-assets** | Not linked to a fixed value; investment objects or digital usage rights | Bitcoin, Ether, utility tokens for access to a platform or game |

ARTs and EMTs aim for a **stable value** (often called **stablecoins**). Bitcoin and similar crypto-assets have no such link and, as the FMA puts it, no stable value.

## Regulated services

According to the FMA, the following crypto services require **MiCAR authorisation**. Authorised providers must meet requirements on transparency, capital, IT security, risk management, client information and conflicts of interest:

- exchanging crypto-assets for official currency, or for other crypto-assets;
- operating a crypto **trading platform**; executing, receiving and transmitting orders;
- **custody**: holding clients' crypto-assets and private keys;
- **advice**, portfolio management and placing new crypto-assets;
- **transfer** services, such as wallet-to-wallet transfers on a client's behalf.

## What remains unregulated

The FMA warns that unregulated offers have **no authorisation, no independent supervision and no duty to inform**:

- **DeFi platforms and crypto lending and borrowing**, often promising very high interest. You may not know who is behind them, and total loss is possible if the platform becomes insolvent.
- **Staking-as-a-service**: providers may disappear with the assets; losses can come from price falls or technical errors.
- **NFTs**: value often purely speculative; forgeries and fraudulent sales are common.
- **Mixed platforms** that show regulated and unregulated products side by side. Investors may wrongly believe MiCAR protects them, and providers may use their licence to promote unregulated products.

> [!WARNING] Crypto and fraud
> Many investment scams ask victims to send money as crypto-assets, for example via bitcoin ATMs, directly into the scammers' wallets (Investor.gov; Category T). Check any provider in the FMA's company database and warning list first.

## Tax in Austria

> [!AUSTRIA] 27.5% since March 2022
> Since 1 March 2022, income from cryptocurrencies has been taxed as capital income at **27.5%**, for those acquired after 28 February 2021 (BMF). Category Q explains the details, and why the rules are technical and can change.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - EU regulators warn that many crypto-assets are highly risky and speculative; total loss is a real possibility.
> - MiCAR distinguishes asset-referenced tokens, e-money tokens and other crypto-assets such as bitcoin.
> - Exchanging, trading platforms, custody, advice and transfers need MiCAR authorisation.
> - DeFi lending, staking services and NFTs remain unregulated, with no supervision or duty to inform.
> - In Austria, crypto income is taxed at 27.5% (assets acquired after 28 February 2021).
