## What counts as cash

In investing, **cash** means money you can reach quickly with almost no risk of a fall in its nominal value:

- **Current and savings accounts:** money available at any time.
- **Fixed-term deposits** (*Festgeld*, *Kapitalsparbuch*): money tied up for an agreed period, usually at a higher interest rate.
- **Very short-term government securities:** in the US, Treasury bills; in Austria, short tenors of *Bundesschatz* (lesson 2).

In the US data from lesson 1, 3-month Treasury bills never had a losing year between 1928 and 2025. Their compound return was **3.4% a year**, the lowest of all the asset classes in the table.

## The Austrian deposit guarantee

> [!AUSTRIA] Einlagensicherung
> According to Einlagensicherung AUSTRIA, balances on accounts and savings books (salary accounts, current accounts, time deposits, savings books) are protected up to **€100,000 per person and per bank**, regardless of how many accounts you have there.
> - In some cases, temporary high balances from the last 12 months (for example from selling a privately used home, or linked to events such as marriage, retirement or redundancy) can be protected up to **€500,000** on application.
> - **Joint accounts:** each legitimated account holder is protected up to €100,000.
> - Every Austrian bank that takes deposits must belong to a protection scheme; savings banks and Raiffeisen banks have their own schemes.

## What the deposit guarantee does not cover

- **Securities** such as shares, bonds and fund units are **not** deposits. They remain **your property** in the custody account, so if a bank fails you can have them transferred to another bank. If the bank cannot return them in breach of its duties, investor compensation covers up to **€20,000** (Einlagensicherung AUSTRIA; Category F).
- Amounts **above the limit** at one bank.
- Bundesschatz is a security of the Republic of Austria (OeBFA), so its safety depends on the Republic, not on a bank.

## Interest and tax

> [!AUSTRIA] 25% on bank interest
> Interest on bank deposits and current accounts is taxed at **25%**, withheld by the Austrian bank as KESt. Interest from bonds is taxed at 27.5% (BMF; Category Q).

## The real risk: inflation

Cash protects you from price swings, not from rising prices. If inflation is higher than your interest rate after tax, your money buys less each year (Category G).

> [!EXAMPLE] After tax and inflation
> €10,000 in a fixed-term deposit at 2.5% for one year.
> - Interest: €250; tax at 25%: €62.50; net: €187.50 (1.875%).
> - If inflation is 3%, the money's purchasing power still falls by about 1.1%.

## What cash is for

- An **emergency fund** and money needed in the next few years (Category M).
- A **stable part** of a portfolio that does not fall when shares do.
- **Flexibility**: money ready when needs or opportunities appear.

> [!MISTAKE] Chasing the highest rate abroad
> Very high deposit rates from unknown providers deserve a check. Is it really a bank with a licence? Which country's deposit guarantee applies? Unusually high, "guaranteed" returns are a classic fraud red flag (Category T). Check the provider in the FMA company database.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Cash is accounts, fixed-term deposits and very short-term government securities.
> - In Austria, deposits are protected up to €100,000 per person and bank.
> - Securities are not deposits but remain your property; investor compensation covers up to €20,000.
> - Deposit interest is taxed at 25%.
> - Inflation is cash's main risk; use cash for emergencies and short-term needs.
