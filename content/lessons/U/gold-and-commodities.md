## Why people buy gold

The FMA notes that in times of inflation, economic uncertainty and geopolitical tension, many people look for safe investments, and gold is traditionally regarded as a **"safe haven"**. But the FMA also stresses two facts that advertising often leaves out:

- Gold itself produces **no ongoing income** such as interest or dividends.
- The gold price can **fluctuate considerably**, and it does not always rise.

## What the data shows

In Damodaran's US dollar data (lesson 1):

| Gold | 1928–2025 | 1972–2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Average yearly return (compound) | 5.6% | 8.9% |
| Volatility | 21.5% | 27.3% |
| Worst year | −32.6% (1981) | −32.6% (1981) |

For comparison, the S&P 500 had a volatility of 17.0% over 1972–2025. Gold also had long losing stretches: in the data, it fell in **five years in a row from 1988 to 1992**, and again in **1996–1998**. In some years it rose when shares fell: in **2008**, the S&P 500 lost 36.6% while gold gained 4.3%.

> [!NOTE] Return without income
> A share's value rests on company profits; a bond's on interest payments. Gold's return comes only from changes in its price, set by what the next buyer is willing to pay.

## Other commodities

**Commodities** are raw materials such as oil, gas, industrial metals and agricultural products. Private investors rarely hold them physically. Exposure usually comes through:

- **Futures contracts:** agreements to buy or sell a specific quantity of a commodity at a set price on a future date (Investor.gov; Category N).
- **Products that track commodity prices**, which may use futures and can therefore perform differently from the commodity's spot price (Category N).

## Ways to hold gold

| Route | What you own | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Bars and coins** | Physical metal | Storage, insurance, buying and selling spreads |
| **ETCs** | A debt security of the issuer | Tradeable on an exchange; check the issuer and whether it is backed by physical metal |
| **Gold mining shares** | Part of a company | Affected by the company's costs and management, not only the gold price |

According to the Wiener Börse, an **Exchange Traded Commodity (ETC)** is an **exchange-traded debt security** that reflects the value of commodities or precious metals. Because it is a debt security, the issuer matters.

## The FMA's warning: discounted gold

> [!WARNING] "Pay now, receive later"
> The FMA warns about offers to buy gold **at a discount** if you pay now and receive the gold later, often sold via online platforms, social media or intermediaries, sometimes with bonus gold or referral rewards. The FMA's points:
> - Your money depends on the **provider's financial health**. If it gets into difficulty or becomes insolvent, in many cases there is **no claim to delivery** of the gold.
> - It is often impossible to check whether physical gold was bought and stored at all; in some fraud cases, customers' money was used for other purposes.
> - Many such business models, especially trading physical gold, are **not subject to effective supervision**.
> - **Total loss** of the money paid is possible.

The FMA describes reputable gold dealers as transparent: prices close to the current gold price, certified bars or coins, and no bait offers or sales pressure.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Gold pays no interest or dividends; its return comes only from price changes.
> - Gold's price has been about as volatile as shares or more, with multi-year losing streaks.
> - Commodities are usually accessed through products that may use futures and can differ from spot prices.
> - ETCs are debt securities, so the issuer matters.
> - The FMA warns that "pay now, receive later" gold offers can end in total loss.
