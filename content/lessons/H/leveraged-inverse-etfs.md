Some ETFs (and similar products) promise to deliver **two or three times** the daily return of an index (**leveraged**), or the **opposite** of it (**inverse**), or a multiple of the opposite. The SEC describes them as specialised ETFs with extra risks for buy-and-hold investors.

## The key word: daily

These products are designed to achieve their objective **on a daily basis**. The SEC warns that their performance over longer periods (weeks, months or years) can **differ significantly** from the stated multiple of the index's performance over the same period, and that this effect can be magnified in volatile markets. The reason is that most of them "reset" every day, and returns compound day after day.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> An index starts at **100**. On day 1 it rises **10%** to 110. On day 2 it falls **10%** to 99.
>
> | | Day 1 | Day 2 | Total over two days |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Index | +10% → 110 | −10% → 99 | **−1%** |
> | 2× leveraged ETF (starts at 100) | +20% → 120 | −20% → 96 | **−4%** (not −2%) |
> | Inverse ETF (starts at 100) | −10% → 90 | +10% → 99 | **−1%** (not +1%) |
>
> The index lost 1%. The 2× product lost 4%, and the inverse product, which you might expect to gain, also lost money.

In markets that swing up and down without a clear direction, this effect can steadily erode the value of leveraged and inverse products, even if the index ends up roughly where it started. In strong one-way trends, the result can also be better than the simple multiple. Either way, it is hard to predict.

## Who uses them

They are mainly tools for short-term traders who monitor their positions closely. The SEC specifically warns buy-and-hold investors about their extra risks and suggests reading the prospectus and understanding these products before investing.

> [!WARNING]
> With leveraged products, losses can be very large and very fast. A 3× product on an index that falls 33% in one day could lose almost everything. Read the key information document carefully, and do not hold these products long-term unless you fully understand the daily reset.

> [!MISTAKE] "If I'm sure the market goes up over the next year, a 2× ETF will double my gain"
> Over longer periods, the result can be much less than double, or even a loss, depending on the path the index takes. The 2× applies to each single day, not to the year.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Leveraged and inverse ETFs aim for a multiple or the opposite of an index's daily return.
> - Because they reset daily, results over longer periods can differ greatly from the multiple you expect.
> - Swinging markets erode their value; losses can be large and fast.
> - They are tools for short-term traders, not for buy-and-hold investors.
