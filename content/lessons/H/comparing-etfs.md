This lesson does not tell you which ETF to buy; Stockora Academy never does. It gives you the questions to ask so you can read fund documents with confidence.

## Where to find the facts

- **Key information document (KID):** a short, standardised document for EU retail investors describing the fund's objectives, risks and costs.
- **Prospectus:** the full legal description of the fund.
- **Factsheet:** a monthly overview with holdings, sector and country weights, and performance.
- **Annual report:** the audited figures.

All of these are published by the fund provider. Marketing pages and social media are not a substitute.

## The checklist

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Which index exactly?** | "World" can mean developed markets only, or developed plus emerging markets. Check the index name, number of holdings, and country and sector weights. |
| **What does it cost?** | TER (running costs), plus trading costs and spreads when you buy and sell. |
| **How well has it tracked?** | Tracking difference over several years, compared with other funds on the same index. |
| **Physical or synthetic?** | Determines whether the fund holds the shares or relies on a swap counterparty. |
| **Accumulating or distributing?** | Affects whether you receive cash, and has tax consequences (next lesson). |
| **Fund size and age** | Very small funds may be closed or merged; a long history gives more data on tracking. |
| **Domicile and legal form** | Where the fund is based (e.g. Ireland, Luxembourg) and whether it is a UCITS fund affects its rules and its tax treatment. |
| **Tax reporting in Austria** | Whether the fund reports its tax data to the OeKB makes a big difference for Austrian investors (next lesson). |
| **Trading** | On which exchanges it is listed, in which currency, and how wide the bid-ask spread typically is. |
| **Currency** | The trading currency is not the same as the currency risk: a euro-traded ETF on US shares still carries dollar risk. |

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Two funds both have "World" in the name.
>
> - Fund A tracks the MSCI World: developed markets only.
> - Fund B tracks the FTSE All-World: developed **and** emerging markets, with considerably more companies.
>
> They are not interchangeable (see the lesson on major indexes). The names alone do not tell you that; the factsheet does.

> [!MISTAKE] "This ETF did best last year, so it's the best ETF"
> Last year's performance mainly tells you how its index did last year. When comparing funds on the **same** index, look at costs and tracking; when comparing different indexes, you are comparing different investments, not better or worse funds.

> [!TIP]
> Make your own comparison table with the same rows for every fund you are considering, filled in from the official documents. It quickly shows real differences behind similar names.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Use official documents: KID, prospectus, factsheet and annual report.
> - First check what the index really contains; names can mislead.
> - Then compare costs, tracking difference, structure, distribution policy, size, domicile, tax reporting and trading.
> - Past performance mostly reflects the index; it is not a reason on its own to pick a fund.
