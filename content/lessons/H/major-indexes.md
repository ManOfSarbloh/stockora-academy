When the news says "markets rose today", it usually refers to one of these indexes. Knowing what each one contains helps you understand what is really being measured, and what an ETF that tracks it holds.

## National indexes

| Index | Country | What it contains | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ATX** | Austria | About 20 of the most liquid shares on the Vienna Stock Exchange | Market value |
| **DAX** | Germany | The 40 largest and highest-turnover German shares on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange's regulated market | Market value |
| **S&P 500** | USA | 500 leading US companies, covering about 80% of the available US market capitalisation | Free-float market value |
| **Dow Jones Industrial Average** | USA | 30 US blue chips, all industries except transportation and utilities | Price |
| **Nikkei 225** | Japan | 225 stocks in the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange | Price |

## Global indexes

| Index | What it covers | Number of members (as of 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **MSCI World** | Large and mid-sized companies in **developed markets** only, about 85% of each country's free-float market value | 1,249 (30 September 2026) |
| **FTSE All-World** | Large and mid-sized companies in **developed and emerging markets**, more than 45 countries | about 4,200 |

## MSCI World, FTSE All-World and S&P 500 compared

These three are among the most popular indexes behind ETFs, but they are quite different:

- **S&P 500:** only US companies.
- **MSCI World:** many developed countries, but **no emerging markets** such as China or India. Because the US is by far the largest developed market, it makes up a large part of the index.
- **FTSE All-World:** developed **and** emerging markets, so it is broader than the MSCI World.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Someone says: "I own the whole world through an MSCI World ETF." That is not quite right: emerging markets are missing, and the index is heavily tilted towards the United States because of its size. Whether that matters depends on what you want, but you should know it.

> [!VERIFY]
> Index sizes, member counts and country weights change all the time. Check the index provider's current factsheet before relying on any number.

> [!MISTAKE] "The Dow is the US stock market"
> The Dow contains only 30 companies and is price weighted. The S&P 500, with 500 companies weighted by market value, is a much broader measure of large US companies.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - ATX (Austria, ~20 shares), DAX (Germany, 40), S&P 500 (USA, 500), Dow (USA, 30, price weighted), Nikkei 225 (Japan, price weighted).
> - MSCI World covers developed markets only; FTSE All-World adds emerging markets.
> - Global indexes are dominated by the largest markets, especially the US.
> - Always check what an index contains before investing in a fund that tracks it.
