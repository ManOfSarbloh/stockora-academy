Two ETFs on the same index can be built quite differently. The fund's documents (key information document and prospectus) tell you which structure it uses.

## How the index is replicated

**Physical replication:** the ETF actually buys the shares in the index.

- **Full replication:** it holds every member in the index weight.
- **Sampling (optimised replication):** for very large indexes, it holds a representative selection, which can lead to slightly bigger deviations from the index.

Many physical ETFs also **lend out** some of their securities to other market participants against collateral and a fee (securities lending, Category F). This earns extra income but adds some risk if a borrower fails.

**Synthetic replication:** the ETF does not (necessarily) hold the index shares. Instead, it holds a basket of other securities and enters into a **swap** with a bank (the counterparty), which promises to pay the index return.

- Advantage: it can track hard-to-access markets closely.
- Risk: **counterparty risk**: if the bank cannot pay, the ETF depends on the value of the basket and collateral it holds. How well you are protected depends on that collateral; check the fund's documents.

## What happens to dividends

- **Distributing** ETFs (also called "income" or "Ausschütter") pay the dividends they receive to investors, typically several times a year.
- **Accumulating** ETFs ("Thesaurierer") reinvest the dividends inside the fund, so the value of each unit grows instead. This makes use of compounding automatically (Category G).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Two ETFs track the same index. The index shares pay 2% dividends in a year.
>
> - With the **distributing** ETF worth €10,000, you receive about **€200** (minus costs) in cash.
> - With the **accumulating** ETF, nothing is paid out, but the fund's value is about **€200** higher than it would otherwise be.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> In Austria, accumulating funds do not avoid tax: for funds that report their tax data to the OeKB, the reinvested income ("ausschüttungsgleiche Erträge") is taxed every year even though you received no cash. See the lesson on ETF taxes below.

> [!MISTAKE] "Synthetic ETFs are scams" or "physical ETFs have no risk"
> Both structures are regulated and widely used. Synthetic ETFs carry swap counterparty risk; physical ETFs that lend securities carry lending risk. Know which one you have and why.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Physical ETFs hold the index shares (fully or by sampling); synthetic ETFs use a swap with a bank.
> - Synthetic ETFs carry counterparty risk; physical ETFs may carry securities lending risk.
> - Distributing ETFs pay out dividends; accumulating ETFs reinvest them.
> - In Austria, reinvested income in reporting funds is still taxed every year.
