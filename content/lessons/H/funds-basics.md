Buying 50 different shares yourself is expensive and complicated. A **fund** does it for you: it collects money from many investors, invests it in a portfolio of shares, bonds or other assets, and each investor owns a proportional **share (unit)** of the whole portfolio.

## How a fund is valued

A fund calculates its **net asset value (NAV)**, typically every business day: the value of all its assets minus its liabilities, divided by the number of units. Traditional investment funds (in the US called mutual funds) are bought from and sold back to the fund at a price based on the NAV.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A fund holds shares worth **€10,000,000**, has **€50,000** of liabilities and **1,000,000** units outstanding.
>
> NAV per unit = (€10,000,000 − €50,000) ÷ 1,000,000 = **€9.95**.

## Active and passive

| | Active fund | Passive (index) fund |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Beat a benchmark index through stock selection and timing | Track an index as closely as possible |
| **Who decides** | A fund manager and analysts | The index rules |
| **Costs** | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| **Risk of underperforming the index** | Yes, a manager can be wrong | Small, mainly costs and tracking differences |

Active funds charge for the work of managers and analysts, which shows up in a higher **total expense ratio (TER)** (Category G). Index funds only follow the rules, so they tend to be cheaper. Whether active managers manage to beat the market after their higher costs is a much-studied question (Category M discusses the evidence).

## UCITS: the European fund passport

In the EU, the main legal form for funds that are easily accessible to retail investors is the **UCITS** (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities), set up under an EU directive. UCITS follow a common set of EU rules, and the EU has adopted measures to make it easier to distribute funds across borders. Many ETFs offered to European retail investors are UCITS funds; the fund's documents say so.

> [!NOTE]
> "UCITS" does not mean safe: a UCITS fund can still lose a lot of money if its investments fall. It means the fund follows a common set of EU fund rules.

> [!MISTAKE] "A fund with a famous manager will beat the index"
> Past outperformance does not guarantee future outperformance, and higher fees are certain. Always compare a fund's results after costs with its benchmark index.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A fund pools many investors' money into one portfolio; each investor owns units.
> - NAV = (assets − liabilities) ÷ number of units, calculated every business day.
> - Active funds try to beat an index; passive funds track it, usually at lower cost.
> - In the EU, UCITS is the main fund type for retail investors, following common EU rules.
