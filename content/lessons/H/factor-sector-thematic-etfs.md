The first ETFs simply tracked broad indexes such as the S&P 500. Today there are thousands of ETFs on narrower slices of the market.

## Factor ("smart beta") ETFs

These track indexes that select or weight shares by characteristics ("factors") instead of pure market value, for example:

- **Value:** cheaper shares by valuation measures.
- **Momentum:** shares that have risen recently.
- **Low volatility:** shares with smaller price swings.
- **Quality:** companies with strong balance sheets and stable profits.
- **Dividend:** high-dividend shares.

FINRA warns that returns of smart beta products can be very different from those of market-cap weighted indexes, that rule-based methods can make a portfolio **less diversified** than expected (concentration risk), and that some of these products are thinly traded with wide spreads. FINRA also points out that performance claims are often based on **back-testing** (applying the rules to historical data), which neither predicts future performance nor perfectly replicates the past.

## Sector ETFs

Sector ETFs hold companies from one sector, for example health care or energy (based on classifications such as GICS, Category C). They let you bet on one part of the economy, but they are much less diversified than a broad index: one bad event for the sector hits the whole fund.

## Thematic ETFs

Thematic ETFs follow a story: artificial intelligence, clean energy, robotics, cybersecurity, ageing populations. Things to be aware of:

- **Concentration:** often a few dozen companies, sometimes heavily overlapping with each other and with broad indexes.
- **Popularity timing:** new thematic funds are often launched after a theme has already become popular and expensive.
- **Theme vs reality:** the index rules may not capture the theme the way you expect, and a theme can develop differently than hoped.
- **Costs:** fees are usually higher than for broad index funds.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You own a world-index ETF and add a technology thematic ETF. Many of the largest companies in the thematic ETF are also among the largest in the world index. Your portfolio is now more concentrated in the same few companies than you may realise.

> [!MISTAKE] "I believe in this trend, so the ETF must do well"
> A good story does not guarantee good returns. If everyone already expects the trend, the high expectations may already be in the price.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Factor (smart beta) ETFs select or weight shares by characteristics such as value, momentum or low volatility.
> - FINRA warns of very different returns from market-cap indexes, concentration risk and possible thin trading.
> - Sector and thematic ETFs are narrow bets, much less diversified than broad indexes.
> - Check how much the holdings duplicate what you already own, and the costs.
