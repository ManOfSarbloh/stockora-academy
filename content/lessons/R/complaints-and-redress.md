Most problems with banks and brokers, such as wrong fees, a badly executed order or unsuitable advice, can be solved. Knowing the right sequence of steps saves time.

## Step 1: complain to the firm

Write to the bank or investment firm, describe the problem, include documents (contract notes, statements, correspondence), and say what solution you expect. Keep copies and note dates. Both the FMA and the banking ombudsman expect you to have tried this first.

## Step 2: the banking ombudsman (Austria)

The **Joint Conciliation Board of the Austrian Banking Industry** (*Gemeinsame Schlichtungsstelle der Österreichischen Kreditwirtschaft*, "Bankenschlichtung") tries to resolve complaints through mediation. According to its website:

- It handles problems arising from banking business with an **Austrian credit institution** (foreign currency loans are excluded).
- You must first have **tried to find a solution with the bank**.
- The procedure is **voluntary and free of charge** for both sides.
- It is conducted in **German**, and in **English** for cross-border cases.
- It **cannot give legal advice**.
- A complaint should include your name and address, the bank, a short description of the facts, your proposed solution, and a statement that you have already tried to resolve it with the bank.

## Step 3: the FMA

The **FMA** accepts **complaints about companies it supervises**, for example if you have already complained to the company and suspect that the rules on handling complaints were not followed, or that supervisory law was breached. Send the company's response with your complaint. The FMA also answers consumer enquiries, but it does not answer abstract legal questions.

> [!NOTE] What the FMA can and cannot do
> The FMA supervises whether firms follow the law. It is not a court and does not decide individual compensation claims. For damages, you may need conciliation or the courts.

## Step 4: legal advice and courts

For larger losses or legal disputes, consider advice from a lawyer, a consumer protection organisation, or your legal expenses insurer. Deadlines can apply to legal claims, so do not wait too long.

> [!WARNING] Beware of fake "recovery" offers and fake regulators
> The FMA warns that fraudsters pretend to be FMA staff, using fake email addresses, phone numbers and documents, to obtain personal data or payments. Genuine FMA email addresses end only in **@fma.gv.at**. Never pay "fees" to recover lost money (Category T).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Always complain to the firm first, in writing, with documents.
> - The Austrian banking ombudsman's procedure is voluntary, free, and requires a prior attempt with the bank.
> - The FMA accepts complaints about supervised companies, especially where rules may have been breached.
> - The FMA is not a court; compensation claims may need conciliation or legal action.
> - Beware of fraudsters posing as the FMA or offering to recover losses.
