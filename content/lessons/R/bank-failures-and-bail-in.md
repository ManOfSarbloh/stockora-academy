In the 2008 crisis, many governments rescued banks with public money (Category K). Since then, the EU has built a framework so that the owners and creditors of a failing bank carry the losses first.

## Bail-out and bail-in

The **Single Resolution Board (SRB)**, the EU resolution authority for the banking union, explains the difference:

- A **bail-out** happens when outside investors, such as a **government**, rescue a borrower by injecting money. In the past, taxpayers assumed the risks.
- A **bail-in** happens when the bank's **creditors** are made to bear part of the burden by having some of their claims written off or converted into shares.

Under the EU **Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive (BRRD)**, bail-in allows debt owed by a failing bank to be **written down or converted into equity**, replicating how creditors would lose in a bankruptcy, while protecting taxpayers and keeping critical functions running.

## Who loses first?

The SRB lists the principles resolution authorities must follow:

1. **Shareholders bear the losses first.**
2. **Creditors** bear losses after the shareholders, in the order of their claims in normal insolvency.

Before any resolution action, the bank's capital instruments must be **written down or converted**. The resolution tools include selling the business, a bridge institution, separating assets and bail-in.

In Austria, the **FMA** is the national resolution authority (lesson 1).

> [!WARNING] What this means for investors in banks
> - **Bank shares** can be wiped out in a resolution.
> - **Bank bonds**, especially subordinated ones and some senior ones, can be written down or converted into shares.
> - "Too big to fail" is no longer a promise that investors will be rescued.

## What about your savings and securities?

- **Deposits** at Austrian banks are protected by the deposit guarantee up to **€100,000 per person and bank** (Einlagensicherung AUSTRIA; Category F).
- **Securities** in your custody account remain **your property** and are not part of the bank's assets. If they cannot be delivered, investor compensation covers up to **€20,000** (Category P).
- These protections are separate from the losses that shareholders and bondholders of the bank itself can face.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - EU rules aim to make owners and creditors, not taxpayers, carry a failing bank's losses (bail-in).
> - Shareholders bear losses first, then creditors in order of their claims (SRB).
> - Bank shares and bonds can be written down or converted in a resolution.
> - Deposits are protected up to €100,000 per person and bank; securities in custody remain your property.
> - The FMA is Austria's national resolution authority.
