## What is insider dealing?

**Insider dealing** means using **inside information** to buy or sell, or to cancel or change orders, for yourself or someone else. The FMA explains that the offence can also be committed by **recommending** securities on the basis of inside information or by **passing it on** to others.

The EU **Market Abuse Regulation (MAR)**, which entered into force on 3 July 2014 and has applied since **3 July 2016**, defines inside information (lesson 2): precise, non-public information likely to move the price significantly. The FMA notes that it also includes confidential information about clients' pending orders that is known to the people executing them.

> [!EXAMPLE] Insider dealing in practice
> - A board member learns that a takeover offer will be announced next week and buys shares in advance.
> - An auditor sees that profits will be far below forecasts and sells shares before the profit warning.
> - A board member's partner overhears the news at home and tells a friend, who buys.
>
> All three use or pass on inside information.

## Primary and secondary insiders

Austrian law (§ 163 BörseG 2018), as explained by the FMA, distinguishes:

- **Primary insiders:** people who have access to inside information as members of the management, executive or supervisory body of the issuer, through their profession (for example auditors or interpreters) or through their stake in the issuer's capital.
- **Secondary insiders:** everyone else who has inside information, for example the partner of a board member or a cleaner.

## Penalties in Austria

The FMA summarises the penalties:

| Type of proceedings | Penalty |
|---|---|
| **Criminal court** (insider dealing and recommendations; intent for primary insiders, knowledge for secondary insiders) | **6 months to 5 years** imprisonment |
| Criminal court, unlawful disclosure of inside information | up to **2 years** imprisonment |
| **Administrative** (FMA) | fine up to **€5 million** or up to **three times the benefit** gained or loss avoided |
| Legal persons (companies), in both cases | up to **€15 million** or **15% of total annual net turnover** |

Where a court is competent, the FMA must report a justified suspicion to the public prosecutor.

## Why the ban matters to you

The FMA explains the economic damage: an insider exploits the trust of investors for personal advantage. If insider dealing went unpunished, investors would lose confidence, pay less for financial instruments and leave the market, raising the cost of capital for all companies. Price quality and trading volume would fall, and in the worst case the market could fail.

> [!WARNING] "Tips" can make you an insider
> If someone tells you about an unannounced takeover or profit warning and you trade on it, you may be committing an offence yourself, even if you are not employed by the company.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Insider dealing means using, recommending on the basis of, or passing on inside information.
> - MAR has applied since 3 July 2016 and defines inside information across the EU.
> - Primary insiders include board members, auditors and major shareholders; anyone else with inside information is a secondary insider.
> - In Austria: up to 5 years' imprisonment, administrative fines up to €5 million or three times the benefit, and fines on companies up to €15 million or 15% of turnover.
> - The ban protects trust in markets and lowers the cost of capital for all companies.
