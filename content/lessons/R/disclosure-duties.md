Markets work best when everyone has access to the same important information at the same time. **Disclosure rules** make companies and their insiders publish what investors need, and make it harder for a few people to profit from secrets.

## 1. Inside information: publish without delay

Under Article 17(1) of the EU **Market Abuse Regulation (MAR)**, issuers must make **inside information** that directly concerns them public **without delay**. The FMA explains the purpose: to prevent the misuse of inside information and to enable investors to make well-founded decisions. These announcements are often called **ad hoc announcements**.

According to Article 7 MAR, as summarised by the FMA, **inside information** is information that is:

- **not public**,
- **precise**,
- related directly or indirectly to one or more issuers or financial instruments, and
- **likely to have a significant effect on the price** if it were made public, because a reasonable investor would probably use it as part of the basis for an investment decision.

> [!EXAMPLE] Typical ad hoc announcements
> A profit warning, an unexpected loss of a major contract, a takeover offer, the resignation of the CEO, or a large write-down. The Wiener Börse lexicon describes a profit warning as an ad hoc announcement that profit will be lower, or the loss larger, than forecast (Category I).

A company may **delay** publication under strict conditions (Article 17(4) MAR): immediate disclosure would harm its legitimate interests, the information does not contradict the issuer's last public statement on the same matter, and confidentiality can be ensured. The FMA must be informed afterwards.

## 2. Regular reporting

Listed companies must publish an **annual financial report** within four months of the year end and a **half-yearly report** within three months (§§ 124 and 125 BörseG 2018; Category I).

## 3. Major holdings: who owns large stakes

Under §§ 130 ff BörseG 2018, anyone whose share of voting rights in an issuer with shares on a regulated market (and Austria as home member state) **reaches, exceeds or falls below** one of these thresholds must report it:

**4%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%, 35%, 40%, 45%, 50%, 75% and 90%**

A company may set an additional 3% threshold in its articles. The FMA explains that the duty also covers financial instruments such as options, futures and CFDs, and voting rights attributed through subsidiaries or voting agreements. The report must be sent to the FMA, the issuer and the stock exchange **without delay, at the latest within two trading days**, and the issuer publishes it.

## 4. Directors' dealings: managers' own trades

Under Article 19 MAR, people with **managerial responsibilities** at an issuer, such as members of the management or supervisory board, and **persons closely associated** with them (for example spouses, dependent children) must report their own transactions in the issuer's shares, debt instruments and related derivatives to the issuer and the FMA. The FMA states:

- The duty applies once a total of **€20,000** in a calendar year is reached, added up without netting.
- Reports must be made **without delay and at the latest three business days** after the transaction.

The FMA offers a database to search these reports.

> [!TIP] How investors use disclosures
> - Read ad hoc announcements on the company's website or via the exchange.
> - Check who the large shareholders are, and whether they are buying or selling.
> - Look at directors' dealings: managers buying with their own money can be a signal, although they sell for many personal reasons too.
> - Remember: by the time you read it, the market has usually reacted (Category L).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Issuers must publish inside information without delay (Article 17 MAR), with limited possibilities to delay.
> - Inside information is precise, non-public and likely to move the price significantly.
> - Large holdings must be reported at thresholds from 4% to 90% within two trading days (§§ 130 ff BörseG 2018).
> - Managers and closely associated persons must report their own trades above €20,000 a year within three business days (Article 19 MAR).
> - Disclosures help investors, but markets react quickly.
