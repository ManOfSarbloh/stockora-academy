**ESG** stands for **environmental, social and governance** factors. Some investors consider them to manage risks, others to align their money with their values. Rules have grown to make sure claims about sustainability are honest.

## The SFDR: explaining, not forcing

The EU's **Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR, 2019/2088)** applies to financial market participants managing money for end investors, such as asset managers, insurers, pension providers and investment firms. The European Commission explains that it requires them to inform investors about:

- how they consider **sustainability risks** that can affect the value of investments ("outside-in"), and
- the **adverse impacts** their investments have on the environment and society ("inside-out").

This information must appear on websites, in pre-contractual documents and in annual reports, for specific products and for the firm as a whole.

> [!NOTE] What the SFDR does not do
> The Commission states that the SFDR **does not force** market participants to consider green criteria when investing. It sets rules that require them to **justify the sustainability claims** they make. A revision of the SFDR is under way, so details may change.

## Greenwashing

**Greenwashing** means presenting a product or company as more sustainable than it is. ESMA has made "promoting transparency and tackling greenwashing" a priority and launched supervisory action on ESG disclosures across the EU in October 2022.

> [!TIP] Questions to ask about an "ESG" or "sustainable" fund
> - What exactly does it exclude or select, and how? Read the pre-contractual documents and the KID.
> - Does it follow a recognised label or index methodology?
> - What does it cost compared with similar funds?
> - Do its actual holdings match its marketing?

## Company reporting

Listed companies' management reports may include a **sustainability statement**, and the annual financial report's responsibility statements refer to sustainability reporting standards where applicable (§ 124 BörseG 2018; Category I). This gives investors more data on environmental and social risks.

## ESG and returns

Whether ESG investing helps or hurts returns is debated, and results depend on the period and the method. ESMA's 2024 report on EU funds found that in 2023, ESG **equity** funds outperformed comparable non-ESG funds, while ESG **fixed income** and **mixed** funds underperformed, and that the costs of ESG funds were lower than or similar to those of non-ESG equivalents. One year proves little either way.

## Ethics beyond the rules

Regulation sets minimum standards. Personal ethics can go further: avoiding certain industries, voting at general meetings (Category A), or engaging with companies. These are personal choices; this academy does not recommend any particular approach.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - ESG means environmental, social and governance factors.
> - The SFDR requires providers to disclose how they consider sustainability risks and adverse impacts, but does not force green investing.
> - Providers must justify the sustainability claims they make; regulators target greenwashing.
> - Check what an "ESG" product actually does, its holdings and its costs.
> - ESG's effect on returns is debated; ESMA's 2023 data showed mixed results by asset class.
