Shareholders own a company, but they do not run it day to day. **Corporate governance** is the system of rules and bodies that ensures the people who do run it act in the owners' and the company's interest.

## Austria's two-tier board

An Austrian stock corporation (*Aktiengesellschaft*, AG) has two separate boards:

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Management board** (*Vorstand*) | Runs the company. Under § 70 AktG, it manages the company **on its own responsibility**, as the good of the company requires, taking into account the interests of the **shareholders**, the **employees** and the **public interest**. It can consist of one or more persons. |
| **Supervisory board** (*Aufsichtsrat*) | Oversees the management board. Under § 95 AktG, it must **monitor the management**, and it can request reports from the management board on the company's affairs at any time. |
| **General meeting** (*Hauptversammlung*) | The shareholders' meeting, which decides on fundamental matters such as dividends and the election of the supervisory board (Category A). |

> [!NOTE] Interests beyond shareholders
> § 70 AktG asks the management board to consider shareholders, employees and the public interest. It does not make the share price the only goal.

## The Austrian Corporate Governance Code

On top of the law, the **Austrian Code of Corporate Governance**, first presented on 1 October 2002, sets out voluntary standards for responsible management and oversight (Category I). Listed companies must publish a **corporate governance report** naming the code they follow (§ 243c UGB), and prime market companies on the Vienna Stock Exchange must comply with a code.

## Tools that support good governance

- **Independent supervisory board members** who can challenge management.
- **Audited financial statements** and an independent auditor (Category I).
- **Transparency** about pay, related-party transactions and managers' own trades in company shares, which must be reported (lesson 2).
- **Shareholder rights** to vote, ask questions and elect the supervisory board (Category A).

> [!TIP] What investors can check
> Read the corporate governance report and the remuneration information in the annual report. Look for independent board members, clear explanations where the company deviates from the code, and how management pay is linked to long-term results.

> [!WARNING] Governance failures cost shareholders
> The Wirecard collapse (Category I) showed how much shareholders depend on effective oversight by boards, auditors and regulators.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Austrian AGs have a management board that runs the company and a supervisory board that monitors it.
> - The management board must consider shareholders, employees and the public interest (§ 70 AktG).
> - The supervisory board monitors management and can demand reports at any time (§ 95 AktG).
> - The Austrian Corporate Governance Code adds voluntary standards; listed companies report on their governance.
> - Investors can read the governance report, pay information and directors' dealings.
