> [!VERIFY] Tax rules change
> Simplified overview for private individuals resident in Austria. Some foreign brokers offer to withhold Austrian tax for their customers; others do not. Check what your broker does, and confirm your obligations with the BMF or a tax adviser.

## The key question: is Austrian KESt withheld?

The BMF explains that KESt is withheld when a **domestic** custodian or paying agent is involved. If your securities account is held **abroad** and no Austrian tax is withheld, the income is not settled at source. The BMF names **gains on investments in a foreign securities account** and **foreign dividends paid by a foreign institution** among the income that must be included in the **tax return**.

The tax rate stays the same: the special rate of **27.5%** (25% for bank deposit interest) applies, separately from your other income.

> [!NOTE] Some foreign brokers withhold Austrian tax
> Brokers based outside Austria may offer Austrian tax reporting or withhold the equivalent of KESt for Austrian customers. Whether and how a particular broker does this differs, and it can change. Read your broker's information carefully, and check your annual statements.

## What you must do

If no Austrian tax is withheld:

1. **Collect the figures** for each calendar year: realised gains and losses, dividends, interest, fund income, and foreign withholding tax.
2. **Calculate gains correctly:** sale proceeds minus acquisition cost, without purchase fees, using the moving average price for repeated purchases (lesson 3). Convert to euros.
3. **Declare** them in the income tax return with the supplementary form **E 1kv** for capital income, via **FinanzOnline** (lesson 8).
4. **Credit foreign withholding tax** on dividends in the amount allowed by the relevant double taxation agreement (lesson 4).
5. **Pay** the resulting tax when the assessment arrives.

> [!EXAMPLE] A year at a foreign broker
> Realised gains €1,500, realised losses €400, dividends €200 (foreign withholding tax €30, allowed by the DTA).
> Net capital income: 1,500 − 400 + 200 = **€1,300**.
> Austrian tax at 27.5%: €357.50, minus €30 credit = **€327.50** to pay through the tax return.

## Accumulating funds abroad

Accumulating funds and ETFs generate **deemed distributed income** every year, even without a payout (Category H). With an Austrian bank, this is handled through KESt; with a foreign broker that does not withhold Austrian tax, you must declare it yourself, usually based on the data reported for the fund to the OeKB.

## Transfers between accounts

The BMF's rules on account transfers (lesson 3) matter when you move between brokers:

- From one **foreign** account to another foreign account: notify the tax office **within one month**.
- From a **foreign** account to an Austrian account of the same person: according to the BMF, for transfers **from 1 July 2026**, the exception from the deemed sale applies only if you notify the tax office yourself.
- Give the new bank your **original acquisition costs**; otherwise flat values may be used.

## Keep records

Keep trade confirmations, annual statements, dividend notes and withholding tax certificates for each year. Without them, calculating the correct gain, or proving your costs, becomes difficult.

> [!WARNING] Not declaring is not an option
> Income on which no Austrian tax was withheld must be declared. The BMF explains that under the OECD/G20 standard, agreed in 2014, tax authorities have exchanged financial account information automatically every year since 2017, so foreign accounts are not invisible.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - At a foreign broker without Austrian tax withholding, you must declare capital income yourself.
> - The special rate of 27.5% still applies, separately from other income.
> - Declare via the E 1kv form in your income tax return on FinanzOnline, crediting foreign withholding tax per the DTA.
> - Accumulating funds generate deemed income that must also be declared.
> - Notify the tax office of certain account transfers, and keep all statements.
