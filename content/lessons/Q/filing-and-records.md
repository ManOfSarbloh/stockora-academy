> [!VERIFY] Tax rules change
> Simplified overview for private individuals resident in Austria. Forms, key figures and deadlines change every year. Use the current forms and explanations on the BMF website and FinanzOnline, or ask a tax adviser.

## Do you need to file anything?

| Situation | Typically |
|---|---|
| All investments at Austrian banks, KESt withheld, nothing to correct | Generally **no** declaration needed: KESt settles the tax (lesson 2). |
| Investments at a **foreign** broker without Austrian tax withholding | **Yes:** declare the income (lesson 6). |
| Losses at one bank, gains at another | **Optional**, to get KESt back via the loss offset option (lesson 5). |
| Low total income | **Optional:** apply for the normal tax rate on all capital income (lesson 2). |
| KESt calculated with flat acquisition costs | **Optional or required**, depending on the case (lesson 3). |

## Where it goes: form E 1kv

Capital income is declared in the supplementary form **E 1kv** to the income tax return (form E 1), usually filed electronically via **FinanzOnline**. The BMF's explanations refer to key figures (*Kennzahlen*) on this form; for example, gains from selling securities since 2016 (Kennzahl 981), losses (891) and KESt withheld (899).

## The bank's tax report

From income received in **2025**, Austrian banks and crypto service providers must provide a **tax report** (*Steuerreporting*) on request, at the latest by **31 March** of the following year. On request, reports can be issued for the past five calendar years (but not before 2025). The BMF explains that:

- it lists all your capital income at that institution, including income not covered by the automatic loss offset (such as bank interest and joint accounts);
- its rows refer to the key figures of form **E 1kv**;
- it shows KESt paid, foreign withholding tax credited, realised losses and the KESt credited back through the loss offset.

The BMF adds that the totals can only be transferred to the tax return in full if **all** income listed in the report is included.

## Reclaiming KESt that was too high

The BMF describes a common case: for shares and funds bought between 2011 and 2012, banks sometimes used the market value on 1 April 2012 as the acquisition cost. If your actual costs were higher, too much KESt was withheld. You can reclaim it in the annual tax return by entering the actual gains (or losses) and the KESt withheld in form E 1kv. The tax office may ask you to **prove** the actual acquisition costs and dates with bank statements.

## Records to keep

- Purchase and sale confirmations (acquisition costs and dates).
- Annual statements and tax reports from each bank or broker.
- Dividend notes and foreign withholding tax certificates.
- For funds abroad: the OeKB data used for deemed distributed income.
- Documents on gifts and inheritances (they keep the previous owner's costs and dates).

> [!TIP] Get help when it gets complex
> Foreign brokers, many funds, crypto, moving abroad or inherited holdings quickly become complicated. A tax adviser (*Steuerberater*) can save you money and mistakes.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - With Austrian banks and correct KESt, usually nothing needs to be declared.
> - Foreign brokers without Austrian withholding mean declaring income in form E 1kv via FinanzOnline.
> - Banks provide a tax report on request by 31 March, from income received in 2025, linked to the E 1kv key figures.
> - Excess KESt, for example from flat acquisition costs, can be reclaimed through the tax return with proof.
> - Keep purchase confirmations and annual statements for every year.
