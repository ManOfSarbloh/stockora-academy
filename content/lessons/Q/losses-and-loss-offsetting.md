> [!VERIFY] Tax rules change
> Simplified overview for private individuals resident in Austria holding investments as private assets. Loss offset rules have exceptions and change over time. Check with the BMF, your bank or a tax adviser before relying on them.

## The rules in brief

The BMF summarises the loss offset rules (§ 27 Abs. 8 EStG):

1. Losses from selling capital assets, from derivatives and from cryptocurrencies can only be offset against **gains and income from private capital assets**, not against your salary or other income.
2. They can only be offset against capital income taxed in the **same way**, and only **in the same year**. Losses on investments taxed at the special rate cannot reduce income taxed at the normal progressive rate.
3. They **cannot** be offset against **interest on bank deposits** or against distributions from private foundations.

> [!NOTE] Same year only
> The BMF describes the offset as taking place in the same year. Losses that cannot be used in that year do not simply carry forward to later years under these rules.

## What can be offset: examples

> [!EXAMPLE] Shares against shares
> In 2026 you sell share A with a gain of **€1,000** and share B with a loss of **€600**, both at the same Austrian bank.
> Net taxable result: €400. KESt: 27.5% × 400 = **€110**, instead of €275 on the gain alone.

> [!EXAMPLE] Dividends also count
> You received €300 in dividends with €82.50 KESt withheld, and later in the year sell a share with a €300 loss. The loss can be offset against the dividend, so the bank credits back the €82.50 (subject to the 15% foreign tax credit rules for foreign dividends).

> [!EXAMPLE] What does not work
> A €500 loss on shares cannot reduce the 25% tax on €500 of savings account interest. And it cannot reduce the income tax on your salary.

## What your bank does automatically

The BMF explains that Austrian custodian banks carry out an **automatic loss offset** for the securities accounts they manage, except for joint accounts, business accounts and trust accounts. If a loss occurs after KESt was paid on earlier gains in the same year, the bank **credits** KESt back to you.

However, the automatic offset only works **within one bank**. It does not combine accounts at different banks, income from foreign accounts, or (automatically) cryptocurrencies with other capital income.

## Offsetting across banks: the option in your tax return

For anything beyond the automatic offset, especially **across banks**, with **foreign** income, or between cryptocurrencies and other capital income, the BMF explains that you can use the **loss offset option** (*Verlustausgleichsoption*, § 97 Abs. 2 EStG) in your **income tax return**. Unlike the option for low incomes, you do not have to disclose all your capital income.

> [!EXAMPLE] Two banks
> Bank 1: gain €2,000, KESt €550 withheld. Bank 2: loss €2,000.
> Neither bank sees the other. In your tax return, using the loss offset option, the net result is €0, and the **€550** KESt can be refunded.

## The tax report

From income received in **2025**, Austrian banks must provide a **tax report** on request, at the latest by **31 March** of the following year. It lists realised losses and the KESt credited through the automatic offset, and helps you fill in the tax return (lesson 8).

> [!MISTAKE] Selling only to "use" a loss without thinking
> Realising a loss to save tax can make sense, but the investment decision should come first. Transaction costs, spreads and the risk of missing a rebound also count (Category O).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Losses on capital assets can only be offset against private capital income taxed in the same way, in the same year.
> - They cannot be offset against bank deposit interest or against salary.
> - Austrian banks automatically offset losses within their own accounts and credit KESt back.
> - Across banks or with foreign accounts, use the loss offset option in your tax return.
> - Ask your bank for the tax report, available by 31 March for income from 2025 onwards.
