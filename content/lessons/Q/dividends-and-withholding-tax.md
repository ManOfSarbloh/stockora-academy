> [!VERIFY] Tax rules change
> Simplified overview for private individuals resident in Austria. Withholding rates abroad, double taxation agreements and Austrian rules can change. Check with the BMF, your bank or a tax adviser, and see the foreign country's official tax authority for refund procedures.

## Austrian dividends

Dividends from Austrian companies are subject to **27.5% KESt**, withheld by the company, which generally settles the tax (lesson 2).

## Foreign dividends through an Austrian bank

Many countries deduct a **withholding tax** (*Quellensteuer*) on dividends paid to foreign investors. When the dividend then reaches your account at an Austrian bank, Austrian tax also applies.

The BMF explains that the Austrian bank can **credit** foreign withholding tax actually paid against the KESt, but the credit may **not exceed 15% of the dividend**, regardless of the double taxation agreement.

> [!EXAMPLE] Foreign withholding of 15%
> Gross dividend: **€100**. Foreign withholding tax: **€15**.
> Austrian tax at 27.5%: €27.50, minus credit €15 = **€12.50** KESt.
> Total tax: 15 + 12.50 = **€27.50**. Net to you: **€72.50**, the same as for an Austrian dividend.

> [!EXAMPLE] Foreign withholding above 15%
> Gross dividend: **€100**. Foreign withholding tax: **€25**.
> The Austrian bank credits at most €15: KESt = 27.50 − 15 = **€12.50**.
> Total tax: 25 + 12.50 = **€37.50**. Net to you: **€62.50**.
>
> The extra €10 can, depending on the country and the double taxation agreement, sometimes be reclaimed from the foreign tax authority. Procedures, forms and deadlines differ by country and can be time-consuming.

## Foreign dividends through a foreign broker

If a **foreign** institution pays the dividend, no KESt is deducted. The BMF states that such dividends must be included in your **tax return**, taxed separately at the special rate of **27.5%**. Foreign withholding tax can be credited in the amount provided for in the relevant **double taxation agreement**. Lesson 6 covers foreign brokers.

## Seeing it on your statements

From income received in **2025**, Austrian banks must provide a **tax report** (*Steuerreporting*) on request, at the latest by **31 March** of the following year. The BMF explains that it shows, for each type of income, the KESt paid and the **foreign withholding tax** credited, using the key figures of the tax return form E 1kv (lesson 8).

> [!TIP] Funds and ETFs
> When you hold foreign shares through a fund or ETF, withholding taxes are paid inside the fund and reduce its returns. The fund's tax treatment in Austria follows the investment fund rules (Category H, lesson 7).

> [!MISTAKE] Ignoring withholding tax when comparing yields
> A 4% dividend yield from a country with high withholding tax can leave you less after tax than a 3.5% yield elsewhere. Look at the net amount.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Austrian dividends: 27.5% KESt, withheld by the company.
> - Foreign dividends via an Austrian bank: foreign withholding tax is credited up to 15% of the dividend.
> - If foreign withholding exceeds 15%, the excess is an extra cost unless reclaimed abroad.
> - Via a foreign broker: declare the dividends in your tax return at 27.5%, crediting foreign tax per the DTA.
> - Austrian banks provide a tax report on request by 31 March, from income received in 2025.
