> [!VERIFY] Tax rules change
> Simplified overview for private individuals resident in Austria holding investments as private assets. Rules, deadlines and transition provisions change. Check with the BMF, your bank or a tax adviser before acting.

## The basic formula

The BMF: the gain on a sale is the **difference between the sale proceeds and the acquisition costs**. A redemption, for example of a bond at maturity, is treated like a sale.

**Purchase fees are not part of the acquisition cost** (§ 27a Abs. 4 Z 2 EStG), and custody fees cannot be deducted.

> [!EXAMPLE] The BMF's own example
> A buys shares through an Austrian bank for **€1,000** and pays **€5** in fees. Until the sale, custody fees of **€20** are charged. A sells the shares for **€1,200**.
>
> Taxable gain = 1,200 − 1,000 = **€200**. Neither the €5 fee nor the €20 custody fees reduce it.
> KESt at 27.5%: **€55**.

## Several purchases of the same security: the moving average price

If you buy the same security (same securities identification number) several times and hold it in one securities account, § 27a Abs. 4 Z 3 EStG requires the **moving average price** (*gleitender Durchschnittspreis*) in euros as the acquisition cost.

> [!EXAMPLE] Buying in three steps
> | Purchase | Units | Price | Cost |
> |---|---:|---:|---:|
> | 1 | 10 | €100 | €1,000 |
> | 2 | 10 | €80 | €800 |
> | 3 | 20 | €110 | €2,200 |
> | **Total** | **40** | | **€4,000** |
>
> Moving average price: 4,000 ÷ 40 = **€100** per unit.
> You sell 15 units at €120: gain = 15 × (120 − 100) = **€300**. The remaining 25 units keep an average of €100 until the next purchase changes it.

This matters for savings plans (Category M), where you buy the same fund every month.

## Old holdings and new holdings

Austria began taxing gains on shares and funds generally only for purchases from 2011. The BMF explains:

- **Old holdings (*Altbestand*):** capital assets acquired **up to 31 December 2010** are in principle **not** subject to tax on gains (exception: holdings of 1% or more in a company).
- **New holdings (*Neubestand*):** assets acquired **from 1 January 2011** are subject to the current rules.
- For bonds and similar debt securities, **31 March 2012** is the relevant cut-off date, with special rules for purchases between 1 October 2011 and 31 March 2012.

Inheritances and gifts do **not** turn old holdings into new ones; the original purchase date and acquisition cost of the previous owner continue to apply.

## When a transfer counts as a sale

The BMF lists events that are **treated like a sale** to prevent tax avoidance:

- **Transfer out of a securities account** or to another account is generally treated as a sale at the market value, unless taxation on a later actual sale is secured. A transfer to another account of the **same person at the same bank** is fine; for other transfers, the bank needs information, for example that it is a gift or inheritance, or an order to pass the data on.
- For transfers **from one foreign account to another foreign account**, the account holder must notify the tax office **within one month**. For transfers from a foreign account to a domestic account of the same person, the BMF states that from **1 July 2026** the exception from the deemed sale also applies only if the taxpayer notifies the tax office.
- **Moving abroad** (*Wegzug*): because Austria usually loses the right to tax later gains, moving abroad is treated as a sale at market value. In certain cases, such as moving to an EU/EEA country, you can apply for the tax to be deferred until an actual sale.

## When the acquisition cost is unknown

If a new bank does not receive the acquisition costs, the BMF explains that it must use the market value at the time of transfer, reduced by 0.5% for each month since purchase (but not below half the market value). KESt deducted on this flat basis does **not** settle the tax: you must declare the actual income in your tax return. Keep your purchase statements.

The BMF also describes how investors can reclaim excessive KESt in their tax return if banks used a flat value as of 1 April 2012 for older purchases whose real costs were higher.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Taxable gain = sale proceeds − acquisition cost; purchase fees and custody fees do not count.
> - Repeated purchases of the same security are averaged: the moving average price.
> - Shares and funds bought up to 31 December 2010 are generally old holdings, exempt from tax on gains.
> - Transfers between accounts and moving abroad can count as a sale; notify banks or the tax office as required.
> - Keep purchase statements: without them, flat values may be used and you may need to correct them in your tax return.
