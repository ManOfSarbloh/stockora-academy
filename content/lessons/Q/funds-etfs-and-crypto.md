> [!VERIFY] Tax rules change
> Simplified overview for private individuals resident in Austria. Fund and crypto taxation are technical and change. Check with the BMF, your bank or a tax adviser before acting.

## Funds and ETFs: taxed every year

Category H explains fund taxation in detail ("How ETFs are taxed in Austria"). The essentials:

- **Distributions** are taxed when paid, at **27.5%**.
- **Deemed distributed income** (*ausschüttungsgleiche Erträge*): under § 186 InvFG, income a fund retains, including **60% of the positive balance of realised gains**, counts as distributed and is taxed **every year**, even if no cash is paid. This is why accumulating ETFs still create a tax bill.
- **Selling** fund units: the gain is taxed. Deemed distributed income already taxed **increases the acquisition cost**, so it is not taxed twice.

## Reporting funds and the OeKB

Funds report their tax data to the **Oesterreichische Kontrollbank (OeKB)**. Banks use these figures to calculate KESt. For funds that do not report, special rules apply that can lead to higher taxation (Category H). Check before buying whether a fund is a **reporting fund**.

> [!EXAMPLE] An accumulating ETF in a year
> You hold accumulating ETF units bought for €10,000. The fund reports deemed distributed income of €200 for your units.
> - Tax: 27.5% × €200 = **€55**, withheld by an Austrian bank or to be declared if your account is abroad.
> - Your tax acquisition cost rises to **€10,200**.

## Cryptocurrencies

The BMF explains that since **1 March 2022**, income from cryptocurrencies has been part of **income from capital assets**, taxed at the special rate of **27.5%**:

- The new rules apply to cryptocurrencies acquired **after 28 February 2021** (*Neuvermögen*). Those acquired **up to 28 February 2021** are in principle old holdings, still taxed under the previous rules.
- The legal definition (§ 27b Abs. 4 EStG) covers digital representations of value used as a means of exchange, such as Bitcoin and Ether, and can include stablecoins. **NFTs** and **asset tokens** backed by real values are **not** covered and are taxed under the general rules depending on their design.
- The BMF notes that domestic crypto service providers have been obliged to withhold KESt **from 2024**.
- Losses on cryptocurrencies can be offset against other capital income, but **not automatically** by banks; only through the tax return (lesson 5).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Fund distributions and deemed distributed income are taxed at 27.5% every year.
> - 60% of a fund's net realised gains count as deemed distributed income (§ 186 InvFG).
> - Taxed deemed income raises your acquisition cost, avoiding double taxation on sale.
> - Funds report tax data to the OeKB; check whether a fund is a reporting fund.
> - Cryptocurrencies acquired after 28 February 2021 are taxed at 27.5% as capital income since 1 March 2022.
