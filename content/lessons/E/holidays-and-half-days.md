Stock exchanges trade from **Monday to Friday**. On weekends there is no trading, and each exchange also has a list of **exchange holidays**. These holidays are not always the same as the country's public holidays.

## Vienna: not every public holiday is an exchange holiday

> [!AUSTRIA]
> In 2026, the Vienna Stock Exchange is **closed** on 1 January, Good Friday (3 April), Easter Monday (6 April), 1 May, the National Day (26 October), 24, 25 and 31 December.
>
> On several Austrian public holidays, however, it **is open**, for example on 6 January, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, Corpus Christi and 8 December.

## New York: closed days and early closes

The NYSE publishes its holidays several years ahead. In 2026, it is closed on ten days, including Good Friday, Independence Day (observed on Friday 3 July), Thanksgiving (26 November) and Christmas Day. On a few days it closes early at **1:00 p.m.**, for example on the day after Thanksgiving (27 November 2026) and on Christmas Eve (24 December 2026).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> On **Friday, 3 April 2026** (Good Friday), both Vienna and New York are closed. On **Monday, 6 April** (Easter Monday), Vienna is closed but New York is open.
>
> So if you hold US shares through a Vienna-based broker, you may still see price moves on days when the Vienna exchange is closed.

## Why holidays matter

- **Orders wait:** an order placed on a holiday is executed (at the earliest) on the next trading day.
- **Settlement shifts:** settlement cycles count business days, so holidays push settlement dates back (see Category D).
- **Half-days:** on early-close days, liquidity is often lower in the afternoon, and closing prices are set earlier.

> [!VERIFY]
> Holiday calendars are set by each exchange, often for one year at a time. Always check the official calendar; this site's Trading Times section shows holidays for the current and next year.

> [!MISTAKE] "It's a public holiday, so the exchange is closed"
> Not necessarily. The Vienna Stock Exchange trades on several Austrian public holidays, and foreign markets follow their own holidays.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Exchanges trade Monday to Friday and close on their own list of holidays.
> - Exchange holidays and public holidays are not the same thing.
> - Some exchanges close early on special days, such as the NYSE at 1:00 p.m. before Christmas.
> - Holidays delay order execution and settlement.
