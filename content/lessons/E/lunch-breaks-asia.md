European and US exchanges trade continuously through the middle of the day. Several large Asian exchanges instead split the day into a **morning session** and an **afternoon session**, with a break in between.

## Three examples (local time)

| Exchange | Morning | Break | Afternoon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tokyo** (TSE) | 09:00 – 11:30 | 11:30 – 12:30 | 12:30 – 15:30 (ends with a closing auction) |
| **Hong Kong** (HKEX) | 09:30 – 12:00 (after a pre-opening auction 09:00 – 09:30) | 12:00 – 13:00 | 13:00 – 16:00, then a closing auction ending at a random time between 16:08 and 16:10 |
| **Shanghai** (SSE main board) | 09:30 – 11:30 (after an opening call auction 09:15 – 09:25) | 11:30 – 13:00 | 13:00 – 14:57, then a closing call auction 14:57 – 15:00 |

> [!NOTE]
> Trading hours change: Tokyo, for example, extended its afternoon session by 30 minutes and introduced a closing auction on 5 November 2024, so the market now closes at 15:30 instead of 15:00.

## What happens during the break

During the break, orders may be accepted (in Tokyo from 12:05 for the afternoon session), but nothing trades. News that comes out at lunchtime is reflected when the afternoon session starts, much like overnight news at the morning open.

## Seen from Vienna

Asian markets mostly trade while Europe sleeps or has its breakfast:

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Tokyo is normally **8 hours ahead** of Vienna in winter and **7 hours ahead** in summer (Japan has no daylight saving time).
>
> Tokyo's 09:00–15:30 trading day therefore corresponds to **01:00–07:30 Vienna time** in winter and **02:00–08:30** in summer. By the time the Vienna Stock Exchange opens at 09:00, Tokyo has already closed (or, in summer, is just closing).

> [!VERIFY]
> Session times, breaks and holidays are set by each exchange and change from time to time. Check the official exchange page, and see the country pages under World Markets.

> [!MISTAKE] "Asian markets are open all day like the US"
> Not necessarily: an order sent during the midday break waits for the afternoon session. And because of the time difference, most Asian trading happens during the European night.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Tokyo, Hong Kong and Shanghai split the trading day into morning and afternoon sessions with a midday break.
> - Tokyo: 09:00–11:30 and 12:30–15:30 since November 2024.
> - Hong Kong: 09:30–12:00 and 13:00–16:00 plus auctions; Shanghai: 09:30–11:30 and 13:00–15:00 including auctions.
> - From Vienna, Asian markets trade mostly during the night and early morning.
