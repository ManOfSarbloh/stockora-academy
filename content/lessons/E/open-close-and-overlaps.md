Trading activity is not spread evenly across the day. Two moments stand out, the **open** and the **close**, and for European investors there is a third: the **overlap** with the US session.

## Why the open is often turbulent

Between one day's close and the next day's open, news piles up: company announcements (often released outside trading hours), economic data, events in other countries, and moves in Asian markets overnight. At the open, all of this has to be priced in at once. That is why exchanges such as Vienna start the day with an **opening auction**: it collects orders first and finds one price instead of letting the first few orders push the price around.

Even after the auction, prices often move more in the first part of the session, as investors react to the overnight news and to each other.

## Why the close matters

Many investors and funds want to trade at the **closing price**: it is used to value portfolios and funds, to calculate indexes, and as a reference for many contracts. Their orders are concentrated into the **closing auction**, which is why the close is often a time of high volume.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> An index fund must buy shares of a company that joins an index "at the close" on a certain day. It sends a large order into the closing auction, together with many other funds doing the same. The closing auction absorbs all of these orders at one price, but that price can differ noticeably from the price just before the auction.

## The Europe–US overlap

Normally, the NYSE core session (15:30–22:00 Vienna time) overlaps with European trading for about **two hours**, from **15:30 to 17:30** Vienna time, when the Vienna Stock Exchange's continuous trading ends. During these hours:

- US news and the US open can move European shares;
- many shares listed in both regions trade on both continents at once;
- activity and volatility can rise in the European afternoon.

During the few weeks a year when the time difference is only five hours (see the previous lesson), the overlap starts one hour earlier, at 14:30.

> [!NOTE]
> These are general patterns, not rules. On any given day, the biggest moves can happen at any time, for example when unexpected news breaks at midday.

> [!MISTAKE] "I'll buy at the open, prices are freshest then"
> The open is often when prices swing most and spreads may be widest, as the market digests overnight news. Some long-term investors therefore avoid placing orders in the first minutes of trading.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Overnight news is priced in at the open, which often makes it turbulent; opening auctions help.
> - The close matters because many valuations and index trades use the closing price.
> - Europe and the US normally overlap from 15:30 to 17:30 Vienna time.
> - These are tendencies, not guarantees.
