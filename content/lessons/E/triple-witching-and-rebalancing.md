Some trading days are special because large amounts of money have to move on that exact day, regardless of the news. Two such events are **derivatives expiries** and **index rebalancing**.

## Triple witching

Many futures and options contracts (Category N) expire on a fixed schedule. In the US, the **third Friday of March, June, September and December** is the day when stock options, stock index futures and options on those futures all expire together. This is known as **triple witching** (sometimes "quadruple witching", if single-stock futures are counted too). Traders and hedgers close or roll over large positions, which can cause abnormally high trading activity.

Europe has similar expiry days: at Eurex, for example, the final settlement day of EURO STOXX 50 index futures is the **third Friday** of each contract month, with quarterly contract months in March, June, September and December.

## Index rebalancing

Indexes are reviewed regularly: companies are added or removed, and the weights are adjusted. Funds that track an index (Category H) must then buy the newcomers and sell the leavers, often at the closing price of the effective date.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> For the ATX, the Vienna Stock Exchange's index committee decides on changes to the **index composition twice a year, in March and September**. The number of shares and other calculation values are reviewed **quarterly** (March, June, September, December). Changes usually take effect after the last trading day of the index derivatives, which is normally the **third Friday** of the month.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A company is added to an index effective after the close on a third Friday. Index funds tracking that index must own the share by then. Many of them buy in the **closing auction** that day, so the share may see a very large volume at the close, many times its normal daily volume.

## What this means for you

For long-term investors, these days usually do not matter much. But be aware that prices around the closing auction on such Fridays can be influenced by large mechanical orders, not by news about the company.

> [!MISTAKE] "A share jumped on index-inclusion day, so the company must be doing great"
> Index inclusion creates mechanical buying by funds. That says something about the company's size and trading, not necessarily about its business prospects.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Triple witching: the third Friday of March, June, September and December, when several US derivatives expire together.
> - European index futures (e.g. EURO STOXX 50 on Eurex) also settle on the third Friday of the contract month.
> - Index rebalancing forces funds to buy and sell on set dates, often at the close.
> - The ATX composition is reviewed in March and September; changes usually take effect after the third Friday.
