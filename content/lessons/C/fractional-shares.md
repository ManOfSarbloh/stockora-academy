Some shares cost hundreds or even thousands of euros each. **Fractional share investing** lets you buy **less than one whole share**, so you can invest an amount of money rather than a number of shares.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A share costs **€1,000**. You want to invest **€100**.
>
> With fractional shares, you buy €100 ÷ €1,000 = **0.1 shares**. If the share rises 5% to €1,050, your 0.1 shares are worth **€105**.

## How it usually works

- You can often place orders in **money amounts** ("buy €100 worth") or in **share amounts** ("buy 0.25 shares").
- You generally receive **dividends** and take part in **splits** in proportion to the fraction you own.
- Exactly how orders are handled depends on the broker. Some brokers collect many customers' fractional orders and execute them together, which can affect the price and timing you get. Some only accept market orders for fractional shares.

## Things to check with your broker

The SEC suggests asking your broker about these points before you start:

- **Voting rights:** you may not be able to vote with fractional shares.
- **Fees:** some brokers charge fees for fractional share trades.
- **Liquidity:** some brokers do not guarantee that you can sell fractional shares at any time, even when whole shares of the same stock trade easily.
- **Transfers:** fractional shares generally **cannot be transferred** to another broker. If you move your account, you may have to sell them, which could have tax consequences.

> [!NOTE]
> Fractional shares make it easier to spread small amounts across many companies, but so do funds and ETFs (Category H), which can be a simpler way to diversify with little money.

> [!MISTAKE] "A fraction of an expensive share is a better deal than a whole cheap share"
> Neither is cheaper. What matters is how much money you invest and what the company is worth, not the price of one share or whether you own a fraction of it.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Fractional shares let you invest a fixed amount of money in any share price.
> - You usually receive dividends in proportion to your fraction.
> - Voting, fees, liquidity and transfers depend on your broker; ask before you start.
> - Fractional shares usually cannot be moved to another broker.
