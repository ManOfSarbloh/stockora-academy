A bank and a bakery are both companies, but comparing them directly makes little sense. To compare like with like, investors group companies into **sectors** (broad areas of the economy) and **industries** (narrower groups within a sector).

## GICS: the most common system

The **Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS)** was developed in 1999 by S&P Dow Jones Indices and MSCI, two large index providers. It has four levels:

| Level | Number |
|---|---|
| Sectors | 11 |
| Industry groups | 25 |
| Industries | 74 |
| Sub-industries | 163 |

The **11 sectors** are:

1. Energy
2. Materials
3. Industrials
4. Consumer Discretionary
5. Consumer Staples
6. Health Care
7. Financials
8. Information Technology
9. Communication Services
10. Utilities
11. Real Estate

Each company is placed in one sub-industry, mainly based on where it earns its revenue. The structure is reviewed every year, and companies can be moved when their business changes.

> [!VERIFY]
> These counts are as of the date this lesson was reviewed. GICS is revised from time to time (the last larger revision took effect in 2023), so check the official GICS page for the current structure.

## Why sectors matter

- **Comparison:** a typical profit margin or valuation in software is very different from one in a supermarket chain. Comparing a company with others in its own industry is much more meaningful.
- **Diversification:** if all your shares are in one sector, one bad event for that sector (for example, a sharp fall in oil prices for energy companies) can hit everything you own at once.
- **Funds and indexes:** many ETFs and indexes are built around single sectors (Category H).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Your five shares are an oil producer, an oil refiner, a pipeline company, an oil-services firm and a petrol-station chain. That looks like five different companies, but all five are in or close to the **Energy** sector. If the oil price collapses, they may all fall together. That is not real diversification.

> [!NOTE]
> "Consumer Discretionary" means things people can choose to buy or postpone (cars, holidays, clothing). "Consumer Staples" means things people buy regardless (food, drinks, household products). This connects to the cyclical and defensive stocks in the previous lesson.

> [!MISTAKE] "Owning many companies means I'm diversified"
> Many companies in the same sector or industry often move together. Diversification means spreading across sectors, countries and asset classes, not just across names.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Sectors and industries group similar companies so they can be compared.
> - GICS, created in 1999 by S&P Dow Jones Indices and MSCI, has 11 sectors, 25 industry groups, 74 industries and 163 sub-industries.
> - Companies are classified mainly by where they earn their revenue.
> - Spreading money across sectors is part of real diversification.
