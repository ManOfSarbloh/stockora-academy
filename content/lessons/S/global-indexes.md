Index names can mislead. Category H introduced the main indexes; this lesson looks at how they divide up the world.

## Developed-only versus all-world

- The **MSCI World** covers large and mid-sized companies in **developed markets** only. Despite its name, it excludes emerging markets.
- Broader families add **emerging markets**. FTSE Russell's paper on its classification process explains that when it expanded coverage, it combined developed, advanced emerging and emerging markets in the **FTSE All-World Index**, covering large and mid-cap companies.
- **Emerging markets indexes** cover only the emerging category; **frontier** indexes cover frontier markets.

Which countries are developed, emerging or frontier is decided by each provider's classification (lesson 1), so the same country can sit in different groups at MSCI and FTSE Russell.

## Regional and single-country indexes

- **Regional:** Europe, Asia-Pacific, North America, Latin America, and so on.
- **Single-country:** for example Austria's **ATX**, Germany's DAX or Japan's Nikkei 225 (Category H).

## Market-cap weighting shapes your exposure

Most global indexes weight countries by the market value of their listed companies (Category H). Countries with large stock markets therefore dominate, regardless of the size of their economies or populations. A single country can make up a large share of a "global" index.

> [!TIP] Check before you buy
> Every index provider and fund publishes **country and sector weights** in its factsheet. Look at the top countries, the top companies and how concentrated they are. Weights change over time.

> [!MISTAKE] "I own a World fund, so I'm in every country"
> A developed-markets "World" index leaves out emerging markets entirely, and a global index may have only small weights in many countries.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - MSCI World covers developed markets only; all-world indexes add emerging markets.
> - Providers classify countries differently, so index contents differ.
> - Regional and single-country indexes, like the ATX, narrow the focus.
> - Market-cap weighting means countries with large stock markets dominate.
> - Always check country and sector weights in the factsheet.
