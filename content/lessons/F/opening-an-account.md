Opening a securities account involves more paperwork than opening a social media account, and for good reasons.

## Step 1: proving who you are (KYC)

Brokers must **"know their customer" (KYC)**: verify your identity, usually with an ID document and sometimes a video call or a bank transfer from an account in your name. This is required by anti-money-laundering rules and helps prevent fraud and identity theft. In the US, for example, FINRA's know-your-customer rule requires firms to use reasonable diligence to know the essential facts about every customer when opening and maintaining an account.

You will typically be asked for:

- name, address, date of birth and nationality,
- an ID document,
- your **tax residence** and tax identification number,
- sometimes your occupation and the source of the money you invest.

## Step 2: questions about your knowledge and goals

Under the EU's MiFID II rules (Article 25), firms must ask questions before certain services:

- **Suitability assessment** (Geeignetheitsprüfung): if the firm gives you **advice** or manages your portfolio, it must obtain information about your knowledge and experience, your financial situation including your ability to bear losses, and your investment objectives including your risk tolerance, so that it recommends only what is suitable for you.
- **Appropriateness assessment** (Angemessenheitsprüfung): for many services **without advice**, the firm must check whether you have the knowledge and experience to understand the risks of the product. If it considers the product not appropriate, it must warn you; if you do not give enough information, it must warn you that it cannot judge appropriateness.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Ben opens an account with an online broker and wants to trade leveraged certificates. The questionnaire shows that he has no experience with derivatives. The broker can let him go ahead without advice, but it must warn him that the product may not be appropriate for him.

> [!WARNING]
> Answer these questionnaires honestly. Exaggerating your experience to "unlock" risky products removes a protection that exists for your benefit.

## Step 3: agreements and documents

You will receive terms and conditions, price lists, information about how orders are executed, and key information documents for certain products. It is worth reading at least the price list and the information about costs.

> [!MISTAKE] "The questionnaire is just bureaucracy, I'll click through"
> The answers decide which warnings you see and, for advice, what can be recommended to you. Click-through answers can lead to products you do not understand.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Brokers must verify your identity (KYC) and your tax residence.
> - With advice, firms must check suitability against your goals, knowledge, finances and risk tolerance.
> - Without advice, firms may check appropriateness, especially for complex products, and warn you.
> - Answer honestly; the questions exist to protect you.
