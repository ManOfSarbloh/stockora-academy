Private investors cannot simply walk up to a stock exchange and buy shares. Exchanges deal only with their **members**, mostly banks and investment firms. To trade, you need a **broker**: a firm that takes your orders, sends them to the market and usually keeps the securities you buy in an account for you.

## What a broker does for you

1. **Executes orders:** passes your buy and sell orders to an exchange or other venue (Category D).
2. **Holds your securities:** keeps them in a securities account, in Austria and Germany called a **Depot**, and passes on dividends and information.
3. **Handles cash:** keeps a cash account (in Austria often called a Verrechnungskonto) for purchases, sales and dividends.
4. **Reports:** sends confirmations, statements and tax documents.

## Types of brokers

| Type | Typical features |
|---|---|
| **Full-service broker / bank with advice** | Personal adviser, research, advice and portfolio management, usually higher fees |
| **Discount / online broker** | Self-service online platform, no personal advice, lower fees |
| **Neobroker** | App-first, simple design, often very low or no commission per trade; product range and order routing vary |

## Bank Depot or broker Depot?

In Austria, many people hold their securities account at their **house bank**, where they also have their current account. Others use a **specialist online broker** or a **neobroker**, which may be based in Austria or in another EU country. The choice affects costs, the range of products and exchanges, and, importantly in Austria, **tax handling** (Category Q explains how capital gains tax works with Austrian and foreign brokers).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Anna buys 20 shares through her bank's online banking. Ben buys the same shares through a neobroker app. Both own the same shares, but they may pay different fees, see different prices (depending on where their orders are executed) and have to deal with tax differently.

## Your securities are not the broker's

Securities in your Depot belong to you, not to the broker. If a bank or investment firm becomes insolvent and cannot return your securities, investor compensation schemes may step in, within limits (see the lesson on investor protection).

> [!MISTAKE] "A zero-commission broker is free"
> Brokers have to earn money somewhere: spreads, currency conversion fees, interest on cash, account fees or other income. Look at the total costs, not only the commission per trade.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A broker executes your orders, holds your securities in a Depot and handles cash and reports.
> - Brokers range from full-service banks to discount brokers and app-based neobrokers.
> - The securities in your Depot belong to you, not to the broker.
> - "Zero commission" does not mean zero costs.
