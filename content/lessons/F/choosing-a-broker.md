Stockora Academy never recommends specific brokers. Instead, here are the **criteria** that matter, in a sensible order. Write down your answers for each broker you consider; a simple comparison table will tell you more than any advert.

## 1. Is the firm authorised and supervised?

This comes first, because fake brokers are one of the most common investment scams (Category T).

- A broker offering services in Austria must be **authorised**, either by the FMA or by the regulator of another EU country (with a so-called passport).
- The FMA's **company database** shows whether a firm has an authorisation and **which services** it may provide. The FMA also publishes **investor warnings** about firms that offer services without permission.
- Fraudsters sometimes **clone** real firms by copying their names and registration numbers. Compare the website address, phone numbers and email addresses with those in the official register, and contact the firm only through the details listed there.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> The FMA's free app gives access to its company database, its register of intermediaries and current investor warnings. Check any unknown provider there before sending money.

## 2. Protection if something goes wrong

Ask which **deposit guarantee** applies to cash held with the broker, and whether the firm belongs to an **investor compensation scheme** for securities. Firms that do not belong to one must tell you so in writing before you sign. (Details in the lesson on investor protection.)

## 3. Total costs

Compare the **total** cost for the way you actually invest:

- commission per trade (fixed and/or percentage),
- custody or account fees,
- **currency conversion** fees for foreign shares,
- spreads and the venues where orders are executed,
- fees for savings plans, transfers and closing the account.

## 4. Product range and markets

Does the broker offer the exchanges, shares, ETFs and order types you need? Can you set up savings plans? Does it offer complex or risky products you do not want to be pushed towards?

## 5. Tax handling

> [!AUSTRIA]
> For Austrian investors, an important question is whether the broker handles Austrian capital gains tax (KESt) for you, or whether you have to declare it yourself in your tax return. Category Q explains the difference, which can matter more than a few euros of fees.

## 6. Platform and service

Is the app or website clear? Are statements easy to understand? Can you reach a human when something goes wrong, in a language you understand?

> [!MISTAKE] "Lots of influencers recommend this broker, so it must be safe"
> Many recommendations online are paid or come from affiliate links. Popularity is not regulation. Check the official register yourself.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - First check that the firm is authorised, for example in the FMA's company database, and watch out for cloned firms.
> - Ask about deposit guarantee and investor compensation.
> - Compare total costs, not just the commission per trade.
> - Check the product range, tax handling and service quality.
> - Never rely on adverts or influencers; check yourself.
