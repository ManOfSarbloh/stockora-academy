## Savings plans

With a **savings plan** (in German: Sparplan), you invest a **fixed amount at regular intervals**, for example €100 on the 1st of every month, automatically, usually into an ETF, a fund or sometimes individual shares. Many brokers allow savings plans from small amounts.

Advantages:

- **Discipline:** investing happens automatically, without having to decide each time.
- **Small amounts:** you can start with little money.
- **No need to "time" the market:** you buy regularly, whatever the price.

## The cost-average effect

Because you invest the **same amount** each time, you automatically buy **more units when prices are low** and **fewer when prices are high**. The Vienna Stock Exchange calls this the **cost-average effect** (Durchschnittskosteneffekt).

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You invest **€100** per month in an ETF:
>
> | Month | Price | Units bought |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 | €50 | 2.0 |
> | 2 | €40 | 2.5 |
> | 3 | €50 | 2.0 |
>
> You invested €300 and own 6.5 units. Your average cost is €300 ÷ 6.5 ≈ **€46.15** per unit, lower than the average price of the three months (€46.67).

> [!NOTE]
> The cost-average effect is real, but it does not guarantee profits. If prices simply rise month after month, investing the whole amount at the start would have bought more cheaply. Category M compares regular investing with investing a lump sum. For many people, the biggest benefit of a savings plan is simply that they keep investing.

**Costs matter:** fees per savings-plan execution can be a large percentage of small amounts. A €1.50 fee on a €25 monthly purchase is 6% of each payment.

## Securities lending

**Securities lending** means lending securities to someone else for a period, usually against collateral and a fee; the borrower must later return securities of the same kind and number. Borrowers often use borrowed shares for **short selling** (Category N).

Some brokers and funds lend out securities held for clients and share part of the income. If your broker does this, check:

- whether you have agreed to it (usually in the terms),
- what collateral protects you,
- how much of the lending income you receive,
- what happens to your voting rights while shares are lent out.

> [!MISTAKE] "A savings plan can't lose money"
> A savings plan is only a way of buying regularly. If the investment falls in value, your savings plan falls too.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A savings plan invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, automatically.
> - The cost-average effect means you buy more when prices are low and less when they are high.
> - Fixed fees can be expensive for small monthly amounts.
> - Securities lending lends shares to others against collateral and a fee; check if and how your broker does it.
