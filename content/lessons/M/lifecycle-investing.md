A 25-year-old saving for retirement and a 63-year-old about to retire face very different risks. The younger saver has decades to recover from a crash; the older one may need the money soon. That is why the mix of investments often changes over a lifetime.

## The glide path

The SEC notes that the most common reason for changing your asset allocation is a change in your **time horizon**: most people investing for retirement hold fewer shares and more bonds and cash as they get closer to retirement age.

A planned path for this shift is often called a **glide path**:

| Years to goal | Example mix (shares / bonds and cash) |
|---|---|
| 30+ | 90 / 10 |
| 20 | 80 / 20 |
| 10 | 60 / 40 |
| 5 | 45 / 55 |
| 0 | 30 / 70 |

*An illustrative example only, not a recommendation. Real glide paths differ widely.*

> [!EXAMPLE] Why reducing risk near the goal matters
> Two savers each have €200,000 and a year like 2008 arrives (in Damodaran's US data: shares −36.6%, 10-year Treasury bonds +20.1%).
> - **Saver A, 100% shares, retiring next year:** about **€126,900** left.
> - **Saver B, 30/70, retiring next year:** 60,000 × 0.634 + 140,000 × 1.201 ≈ **€206,200**.
>
> In 2022, however, shares and bonds fell together, and Saver B would also have lost about 18%. Reducing share exposure lowers risk; it does not remove it.

## Target-date (lifecycle) funds

Investor.gov describes a **target-date fund**, also called a **lifecycle fund**, as a diversified fund that **automatically shifts towards a more conservative mix** as it approaches a particular year in the future, its "target date". You pick the fund whose target date matches your goal; the managers make the decisions on asset allocation, diversification and rebalancing. Their names usually include the year, such as "Target 2045".

### What to check before relying on one

- **The glide path:** how much is in shares now, and how fast does it fall? Two funds with the same target year can be very different.
- **What happens at the target date:** does the fund keep adjusting afterwards, or stay fixed?
- **Costs:** you pay for the automatic management (lesson 6, Category H).
- **The contents:** which markets, which bonds, how much in your home country?

> [!TIP] Doing it yourself
> You can follow a glide path yourself with a few broad funds, adjusting the split every year or two. A written plan (lesson 9) keeps you on track.

## Other life events

The mix may also need to change when your **risk tolerance, financial situation or goal** changes (SEC): a new job, an inheritance, a home purchase, children, illness. Review your plan after such events.

> [!MISTAKE] Thinking retirement is the end of the time horizon
> Money for retirement may need to last 20 or 30 more years. Moving everything into cash at retirement can create a different risk: inflation slowly eating away the purchasing power of savings (Category G).

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Many investors reduce their share of stocks as a goal approaches: a glide path.
> - Reducing risk near the goal can prevent a crash from hitting just before you need the money.
> - Target-date (lifecycle) funds shift automatically towards a more conservative mix.
> - Check a target-date fund's glide path, contents and costs.
> - Review your mix after major life events.
