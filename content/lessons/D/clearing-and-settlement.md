When your buy order is executed, the trade is agreed, but you do not own the shares yet, and the seller does not have your money yet. That exchange happens a little later, in two steps called **clearing** and **settlement**.

## Clearing: making sure the trade will happen

**Clearing** covers everything between the trade and its final completion: confirming the details, working out who owes what, and managing the risk that one side fails to deliver.

On many exchanges, a **central counterparty (CCP)** steps into every trade: it becomes the **buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer**. If one side defaults, the CCP still makes sure the other side gets what it is owed. To be able to do this, it collects collateral (margin) from its members.

> [!AUSTRIA]
> For the Vienna Stock Exchange, the central counterparty is **CCP Austria (CCP.A)**, set up by the OeKB and Wiener Börse. It has acted as central counterparty for all trades on the Vienna Stock Exchange since 1 January 2005. Settlement takes place at **OeKB CSD**, the Austrian central securities depository.

## Settlement: shares and money change hands

**Settlement** is the actual exchange: the shares are moved to the buyer's account and the money to the seller's account, at the central securities depository (CSD). This is done "delivery versus payment": the shares are only delivered if the money is paid, and the other way round.

## T+1, T+2: the settlement cycle

The time between the trade date (**T**) and the settlement date is the **settlement cycle**.

- **T+2** means settlement two business days after the trade.
- **T+1** means one business day after the trade.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You buy shares on a **Monday** (T).
>
> - Under **T+2**, the trade settles on **Wednesday**: that is when the shares are finally yours and the seller gets the money.
> - Under **T+1**, it settles on **Tuesday**.
>
> If Tuesday is a public holiday, settlement moves to the next business day.

Why not settle instantly? Banks, brokers and custodians in many countries need time to confirm details, arrange currency and move securities. Shorter cycles reduce risk (less can go wrong in between) but leave less time to fix errors.

| Market | Standard settlement cycle (as of October 2026) |
|---|---|
| United States | **T+1** since 28 May 2024 |
| European Union (incl. Vienna) | **T+2**, moving to **T+1 on 11 October 2027** (Regulation (EU) 2025/2075) |

> [!VERIFY]
> Settlement cycles are changing in several markets. Check the current rules for the market you are trading in.

> [!MISTAKE] "The trade is finished the moment my order is executed"
> Execution is the agreement. The trade is only completed at settlement, one or two business days later. Your broker usually shows the new position right away, but some things, such as whether you are entitled to an upcoming dividend (Category V), depend on these dates.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Clearing confirms a trade and manages the risk that someone fails; settlement is the final exchange of shares and money.
> - A central counterparty becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer.
> - Settlement happens at a central securities depository, delivery versus payment.
> - The US settles T+1 since May 2024; the EU is moving from T+2 to T+1 on 11 October 2027.
