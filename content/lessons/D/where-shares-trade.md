A popular share is often not traded in just one place. Its trading is spread over several venues, which is called **fragmentation**. Here is the landscape, using the EU and US terms you will meet.

## Lit venues

**"Lit"** venues show their order books to the market before trades happen (pre-trade transparency):

- **Regulated markets (exchanges):** the classic stock exchanges.
- **Multilateral trading facilities (MTFs):** EU trading systems, run by investment firms or market operators, that also bring many buyers and sellers together under fixed rules. Some MTFs compete directly with exchanges for trading in the same shares.

## Dark pools

**Dark pools** are trading systems where participants place orders **without publicly displaying** their size and price. They were designed, in general, to let institutional investors trade large blocks anonymously, without showing their hand and moving the price against themselves.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A pension fund wants to sell **2 million shares** of a company that normally trades 500,000 shares a day. If it put the whole order into a lit order book, everyone would see it and the price could drop before the fund finishes selling. In a dark pool, it can look for a buyer without revealing the order in advance.

Because dark pools do not show orders before trading, they do not contribute to public price discovery until trades are executed and reported. In the US, many dark pools are operated as **alternative trading systems (ATSs)**: SEC-regulated electronic systems that match orders but are not registered as national exchanges.

## Systematic internalisers and OTC

- A **systematic internaliser** (EU term) is an investment firm that trades against its clients' orders on its own account, outside a trading venue, on a regular and substantial basis.
- **Over-the-counter (OTC)** trades are agreed bilaterally between two parties, not on a venue.

Retail orders placed through a broker may be executed on an exchange, an MTF, with a systematic internaliser or with a market-making firm, depending on the broker's order routing.

> [!NOTE]
> How your broker decides where to send your order ("best execution"), and the debate about payment for order flow, is covered in Category F.

> [!MISTAKE] "Dark pools are illegal or secret markets"
> Dark pools are regulated trading systems. "Dark" refers to the lack of pre-trade transparency, not to legality. In the US, for example, trades in listed stocks on dark pools must be reported afterwards and appear on the public consolidated tape.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - The same share can trade on several venues at once (fragmentation).
> - Lit venues (exchanges, MTFs) show orders before trading; dark pools do not.
> - Dark pools mainly help institutions trade large orders without moving the price.
> - Systematic internalisers and OTC trades happen off venues; brokers decide where to route orders under best-execution rules.
