## Tick size: the smallest price step

A share's price cannot change by any amount. It moves in minimum steps called **ticks**. If the tick size is €0.01, a share can trade at €10.00 or €10.01, but not at €10.005.

Tick sizes matter more than they seem:

- **Too large** a tick forces a wide minimum spread, which makes trading more expensive.
- **Too small** a tick lets traders jump ahead of each other by tiny amounts, which can make order books thinner and noisier.

> [!NOTE]
> In the EU, a harmonised **tick size regime** for shares has applied since January 2018 under MiFID II (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/588, known as RTS 11). The minimum tick depends on two things: the share's **price** and its **liquidity**, measured by the average daily number of transactions on its most liquid EU venue. More liquid shares get smaller ticks, because many traders already compete on price.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Two shares both trade at around €25:
>
> - a very liquid blue chip might have a tick of €0.005 or €0.01;
> - a rarely traded small company might have a larger tick, such as €0.05.
>
> (These numbers only illustrate the principle; the exact values come from the official tick size tables.)

## Lot size: the minimum quantity

On most European exchanges, you can buy **one single share**. Some markets, however, trade in fixed **lots** or trading units: you can only buy multiples of a set number of shares. Where this is the case, it is covered on the country pages in World Markets.

## Fees: what a trade really costs

A share purchase involves several layers of costs:

| Cost | Who charges it | Typical form |
|---|---|---|
| **Broker commission** | Your broker | Fixed amount per trade and/or a percentage |
| **Exchange and clearing fees** | Exchange and CCP, charged to their members | Usually passed on to you within the broker's fees |
| **Spread** | Built into the price | Difference between bid and ask |
| **Taxes** | Some countries | E.g. transaction taxes or stamp duties (Category Q) |

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You buy shares for **€500** with a broker that charges **€5 per trade**, and the spread costs you about **€1**.
>
> Total cost: about €6, which is **1.2%** of your investment, before the share has moved at all. Investing €5,000 in one trade with the same fees, the cost would be about 0.1% (€5 + the spread on the larger amount).

The lesson: for small amounts, fixed fees hurt. Category G covers costs in detail.

> [!MISTAKE] "Trading costs are tiny, so I can trade as often as I like"
> Each trade may look cheap, but spreads and fees are paid every time. Frequent trading in small amounts can cost several percent a year.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A tick is the minimum price step; in the EU it depends on a share's price and liquidity.
> - Some markets require fixed trading lots; most European exchanges allow single shares.
> - Trading costs include broker fees, exchange and clearing fees, the spread and sometimes taxes.
> - Fixed fees weigh heavily on small trades.
