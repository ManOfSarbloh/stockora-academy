Before looking at any single candle, chart readers ask a simpler question: **which way is the market moving overall?**

## Market structure

![Uptrend with higher highs and higher lows, downtrend with lower highs and lower lows, and a sideways range between support and resistance zones](./img/trend-structure.svg)

- **Uptrend:** a series of **higher highs** and **higher lows**: each rally goes further than the last, each dip stops higher.
- **Downtrend:** **lower highs** and **lower lows**.
- **Range (sideways market):** the price moves back and forth between roughly the same high and low.

A trend is only clear on a given **timeframe**: a daily uptrend can contain several hourly downtrends.

## Trendlines and channels

A **trendline** connects two or more lows in an uptrend (or highs in a downtrend). A **channel** adds a parallel line on the other side. They help you see the slope of a trend, but they are drawn by hand: two people can draw different lines on the same chart.

## Support and resistance

- **Support** is a price area where a falling price has tended to stop or turn up.
- **Resistance** is a price area where a rising price has tended to stop or turn down.

Common places where traders look for them:

- **previous highs and lows**, including 52-week highs and lows;
- **round numbers** such as €50 or €100;
- **moving averages** (lesson 8);
- **trendlines**.

> [!NOTE] Zones, not lines
> Prices rarely turn at exactly the same cent. Treat support and resistance as **zones** a little above and below a level, and expect the price to poke through them now and then.

**Role reversal:** once resistance is clearly broken, it often acts as support on a later dip, and broken support often acts as resistance. Traders call the return to the old level a **retest**.

## Breakouts, fakeouts and pullbacks

- **Breakout:** the price moves decisively beyond a support or resistance zone.
- **False breakout (fakeout):** the price moves beyond the zone, then quickly falls back inside. Fakeouts are common, especially on short timeframes and on low volume.
- **Pullback:** a temporary move against the trend, for example back towards a broken resistance.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A share has turned down near **€50** three times in two months (resistance). One day it closes at **€51.50**, on twice its usual volume. Some traders treat this as a breakout; others wait to see whether €50 now holds as support on the next dip. If the price drops back to €48 two days later, the breakout was false.

## Volume as confirmation (and its limits)

Traders often look for **higher volume** on a breakout as a sign of real participation, and treat a breakout on thin volume with suspicion. Volume helps, but it does not guarantee that a move will continue.

> [!MISTAKE] "Support at €50 means the price cannot fall below €50"
> Support only describes where buyers stepped in **before**. Any news can push the price through it. That is why traders decide in advance what they will do if the level breaks.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Uptrend = higher highs and higher lows; downtrend = lower highs and lower lows; otherwise a range.
> - Support and resistance are zones where the price turned before: previous highs/lows, round numbers, moving averages, trendlines.
> - Broken resistance often becomes support (and vice versa).
> - False breakouts are common; volume helps confirm but guarantees nothing.
