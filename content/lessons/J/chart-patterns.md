Candlestick patterns cover one to three candles. **Chart patterns** are larger shapes that form over weeks or months. Technical analysts sort them into **reversal** patterns (the trend may turn) and **continuation** patterns (the trend may resume after a pause).

![Simplified sketches of a head and shoulders, a double top, an ascending triangle and a bull flag](./img/chart-patterns.svg)

## Reversal patterns

- **Head and shoulders:** after a rise, a high (left shoulder), a higher high (head) and a lower high (right shoulder). The line through the lows between them is the **neckline**. Traders traditionally treat a break below the neckline as the reversal signal. The **inverse** head and shoulders is the mirror image after a fall.
- **Double top / double bottom:** two highs (or lows) at about the same level. The idea is that the market failed twice to go further. The signal traders usually wait for is a break of the low (or high) **between** the two tops (or bottoms).
- **Triple tops and bottoms** follow the same idea with three attempts.

## Continuation patterns

- **Triangles:** the range narrows as highs and lows converge. Traditionally, an **ascending** triangle (flat top, rising lows) is read as bullish, a **descending** triangle (flat bottom, falling highs) as bearish, and a **symmetrical** triangle can break either way.
- **Rectangles:** the price moves sideways between a flat top and a flat bottom (consolidation).
- **Flags and pennants:** a sharp move (the "pole"), then a short pause that slopes slightly against it (flag) or forms a small triangle (pennant), then often a continuation.

## Why they are harder than they look

- **They are only certain once complete.** A double top is just a "high, dip, high" until the price breaks the low between the tops.
- **Subjectivity:** two analysts can see different patterns on the same chart. Lo, Mamaysky and Wang (2000) built a computer method precisely because the shapes are often "in the eyes of the beholder". Their study found that some patterns carried incremental information on US stocks, which is not the same as a reliable trading signal.
- **Failed patterns:** a pattern that breaks out and then reverses (a "busted" pattern) is common.

> [!MISTAKE] "I can see a head and shoulders forming, so I'll sell now"
> Until the neckline breaks, there is no head and shoulders, just a series of highs. Many apparent patterns never complete.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Chart patterns are larger shapes: reversal (head and shoulders, double tops/bottoms) and continuation (triangles, rectangles, flags).
> - The traditional signal usually comes from a break of a key line (neckline, the low between tops, a triangle side).
> - Patterns are subjective, only clear in hindsight, and often fail.
