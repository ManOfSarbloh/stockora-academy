Multi-candle patterns combine two or three candles into a small story: one side was in control, then something changed. As in the previous lesson, grey candles show the context and the coloured ones are the pattern. The tested figures come from Thomas Bulkowski's studies of US stocks and describe the **direction** of the following move only, not profits after costs.

## Engulfing patterns

![Bullish engulfing: example drawing](./img/pattern-bullish-engulfing.svg)

- **Bullish engulfing:** after a decline, a falling candle is followed by a rising candle whose body completely covers the first body. Traditionally a possible bullish reversal (tested: 63% bullish reversal).
- **Bearish engulfing:** the mirror image after a rise (tested: 79% bearish reversal).
- **Confirmation traders wait for:** further movement in the new direction. **Failure:** the price returns beyond the pattern's extreme.

![Bearish engulfing: example drawing](./img/pattern-bearish-engulfing.svg)

## Harami and harami cross

![Bullish harami: example drawing](./img/pattern-bullish-harami.svg)

- **Harami** ("pregnant" in Japanese): a long candle followed by a small candle of the opposite colour whose body sits inside the first body. Read as "the strong move has paused".
- **Harami cross:** the second candle is a doji.
- **How they fail:** the pause is often just a breather. In Bulkowski's tests the bearish harami was followed by a **continued rise** 53% of the time, and the bullish harami cross by a **continued decline** 55% of the time.

![Bearish harami: example drawing](./img/pattern-bearish-harami.svg)

## Piercing line and dark cloud cover

![Piercing line: example drawing](./img/pattern-piercing-line.svg)

- **Piercing line:** after a decline, a long falling candle, then a rising candle that opens below the first candle's low and closes above the middle of the first body (tested: 64% bullish reversal).
- **Dark cloud cover:** the mirror image after a rise: the second candle opens above the first's high and closes below the middle of its body (tested: 60% bearish reversal).

![Dark cloud cover: example drawing](./img/pattern-dark-cloud-cover.svg)

## Morning star and evening star

![Morning star: example drawing](./img/pattern-morning-star.svg)

- **Morning star:** three candles after a decline: a long falling candle, a small "star" lower down, then a long rising candle that closes well into the first body (tested: 78% bullish reversal).
- **Evening star:** the mirror image after a rise (tested: 72% bearish reversal).

![Evening star: example drawing](./img/pattern-evening-star.svg)

## Three white soldiers and three black crows

![Three white soldiers: example drawing](./img/pattern-three-white-soldiers.svg)

- **Three white soldiers:** three long rising candles in a row, each opening within the previous body and closing near its high.
- **Three black crows:** three long falling candles in a row.
- **Caution:** after three strong candles the market has already moved a lot. Whoever acts on the pattern buys or sells after the move, not before it.

![Three black crows: example drawing](./img/pattern-three-black-crows.svg)

## Tweezers and the inside bar

![Tweezer top: example drawing](./img/pattern-tweezer-top.svg)

- **Tweezer top / bottom:** two candles with (almost) the same high or the same low: the price failed twice at the same level. In Bulkowski's tests, both tweezer patterns more often saw the trend **continue** than reverse.
- **Inside bar:** a candle whose high and low are both inside the previous candle's range. The market is contracting; traders watch which side breaks first, and false breaks are common.

![Inside bar: example drawing](./img/pattern-inside-bar.svg)

## Gaps

A **gap** is an empty space between one candle and the next, when the price opens far away from the previous close (often after news). Four kinds are commonly distinguished:

- **Area (common) gap:** an ordinary gap inside a trading range, often "filled" (the price returns to close it) soon afterwards.
- **Breakaway gap:** the price leaves a range or pattern with a gap, at the start of a new move.
- **Continuation (runaway) gap:** a gap in the middle of an existing strong move.
- **Exhaustion gap:** a final gap near the end of a move, after which the price reverses.

The problem: which kind of gap it was is usually only clear **afterwards**.

> [!MISTAKE] "The pattern is complete, so the move is guaranteed"
> Even the best-tested patterns fail a large share of the time, and the figures above say nothing about the size of the move or the cost of trading. Patterns describe what traders believe; they are not a law of the market.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Engulfing, harami, piercing/dark cloud, stars and soldiers/crows are the classic multi-candle patterns.
> - Each has a traditional reading, a confirmation traders wait for and a typical way of failing.
> - Some patterns behaved the opposite way in large tests; tested figures measure direction, not profit.
> - Gaps are named area, breakaway, continuation or exhaustion, but are usually classified with hindsight.
