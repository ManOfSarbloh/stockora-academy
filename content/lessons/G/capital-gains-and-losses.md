There are two ways to make money with shares: the **price can rise** (capital gain) and the company can **pay dividends** (next lessons). This lesson is about the first.

## Gain and loss in euros

**Capital gain (or loss) = selling price − buying price** (per share), multiplied by the number of shares, minus costs.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You buy **50 shares at €80** and later sell them at **€100**. Fees are **€4** in total.
>
> - Price gain: 50 × (€100 − €80) = **€1,000**
> - After fees: €1,000 − €4 = **€996** (before tax)

If the price had fallen to €70 instead, you would have a **capital loss** of 50 × €10 = €500, plus the €4 fees.

## Gain and loss in percent

To compare investments of different sizes, use percentages:

**Return in % = (selling price − buying price) ÷ buying price × 100**

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Bought at €80, sold at €100: (€100 − €80) ÷ €80 = 0.25 = **+25%**.
> Bought at €80, now at €60: (€60 − €80) ÷ €80 = −0.25 = **−25%**.

Note that a −25% loss needs **more** than +25% to recover: from €60, the price must rise by €20, which is +33%. This asymmetry of losses is explained in Category P.

## Realised and unrealised

- An **unrealised** (paper) gain or loss exists while you still hold the share: it changes every day with the price.
- It becomes **realised** only when you sell. Then it counts for your actual result and, in most countries, for tax (Category Q).

> [!MISTAKE] "It's only a loss if I sell"
> An unrealised loss is still a loss of value: your shares are worth less than you paid. Not selling does not make the loss disappear; it only means you are still exposed to further rises or falls. Decisions should be based on what you think of the investment now, not on what you paid.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Capital gain or loss = (selling price − buying price) × number of shares, minus costs.
> - Percentage return = price change ÷ buying price.
> - A percentage loss needs a larger percentage gain to recover.
> - Gains are unrealised until you sell; realised gains are what count for tax.
