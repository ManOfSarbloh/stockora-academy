Every investment carries costs. Some are visible on your statement, others are hidden in prices. Unlike returns, costs are **certain**: you pay them whether your investment does well or not.

## The main costs

| Cost | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Commission** | What your broker charges per trade, as a fixed amount and/or a percentage |
| **Spread** | The difference between buying and selling price (Category D) |
| **Currency conversion** | A fee or mark-up when you buy shares in another currency |
| **Custody fee** | A fee for keeping your securities account (Depotgebühr), often a percentage per year or a fixed amount |
| **Fund costs (TER)** | For funds and ETFs: the yearly costs charged to the fund, shown as the **total expense ratio** |
| **Taxes** | Capital gains and dividend taxes, sometimes transaction taxes (Category Q) |

## The total expense ratio (TER)

The **TER** shows the costs charged to a fund over its last financial year, as a percentage of the fund's assets: mainly the management fee, custody fees, adviser and audit costs and other expenses. It is deducted inside the fund, so you never see it as a separate bill, but it reduces your return every year. Trading costs within the fund and your own purchase costs come on top.

## Small differences, big effects

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You invest **€10,000** for **30 years** in two funds that both earn 6% per year before costs.
>
> | Fund | TER | Value after 30 years |
> |---|---|---|
> | A | 0.2% per year | about **€54,300** |
> | B | 1.5% per year | about **€37,500** |
>
> A difference of 1.3 percentage points per year costs about **€16,800**: almost a third of the result, because costs compound just like returns.

## Your right to know the costs

> [!NOTE]
> Under the EU's MiFID II rules (Article 24), investment firms must give you information about **all costs and related charges** in good time, aggregated so that you can understand the **overall cost and its cumulative effect on your return**, and at least annually during the investment where applicable. You can ask for an itemised breakdown.

## Watch out for

- **Currency fees:** buying a US share through a euro account usually involves a currency conversion on the way in and on the way out.
- **Account and inactivity fees:** some brokers charge them; check the price list.
- **Product costs:** structured products and actively managed funds often have higher built-in costs than you see at first glance.

> [!MISTAKE] "1.5% a year sounds tiny"
> Over decades, a cost of 1.5% a year can consume a large share of your total gains, because it is charged on the whole amount, every single year.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Costs include commissions, spreads, currency fees, custody fees, fund costs (TER) and taxes.
> - The TER shows a fund's yearly running costs as a percentage of its assets.
> - Costs compound over time; small differences become large.
> - In the EU, firms must disclose all costs and their effect on your return.
