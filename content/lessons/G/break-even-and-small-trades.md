When you buy a share, you start slightly "underwater": you paid fees and the spread, and you will pay fees again when you sell. The **break-even price** is the selling price at which you get exactly your money back.

## Calculating the break-even price

**Break-even price per share = (total amount paid to buy + selling fees) ÷ number of shares**

You must be able to **sell** at that price, which means the **bid** must reach it.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> You buy **100 shares**. The bid is €50.00 and the ask is **€50.05**, so you buy at €50.05. The broker charges **€5** per trade.
>
> - Amount paid: 100 × €50.05 + €5 = **€5,010**
> - To get €5,010 back after the €5 selling fee, you need €5,015 from the sale.
> - Break-even price: €5,015 ÷ 100 = **€50.15**
>
> The bid has to rise from €50.00 to €50.15, a **0.3%** increase, before you are even.

## Why small trades are expensive

Fixed fees weigh much more heavily on small amounts:

| Amount invested | Buy fee | Sell fee | Fees as % of amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| €200 | €5 | €5 | **5%** |
| €1,000 | €5 | €5 | 1% |
| €10,000 | €5 | €5 | 0.1% |

> [!EXAMPLE]
> With €200 and €5 fees each way, the share must rise **5%** just to cover the fees, before any spread or tax. Trading such amounts often can cost more than the shares can realistically earn.

## Ways to keep costs down

- Trade less often, in larger amounts rather than many small ones.
- Compare price lists, including fees for savings plans if you invest small amounts regularly.
- Use limit orders in illiquid shares to avoid paying wide spreads.
- Remember taxes: in many countries, gains are taxed, which raises the real break-even further (Category Q).

> [!MISTAKE] "I sold at the same price I bought, so I broke even"
> Not after costs. You paid the spread and fees both ways, so selling at your purchase price means a loss.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Break-even price = (amount paid including fees + selling fees) ÷ number of shares.
> - The bid, not the last price, must reach the break-even price.
> - Fixed fees can make small trades very expensive in percentage terms.
> - Fewer, larger trades and attention to spreads keep costs down.
