**Inflation** is the general rise in prices over time. When prices rise, each euro buys less. To know whether your investments really made you better off, you have to compare your returns with inflation.

## Nominal and real returns

- **Nominal return:** the return in euros, as shown on your statement.
- **Real return:** the return after taking inflation into account, which shows the change in your **purchasing power**.

A quick approximation: **real return ≈ nominal return − inflation**.
The exact formula: **real return = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1**.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> Your investment earns **5%** in a year in which prices rise by **3%**.
>
> - Approximation: 5% − 3% = **2%**.
> - Exact: 1.05 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = **1.94%**.
>
> You have 5% more euros, but those euros buy only about 1.9% more than before.

## How inflation is measured in the euro area

The European Central Bank (ECB) measures inflation in the euro area with the **Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP)**. Its goal is price stability, which it defines as **2% inflation over the medium term**, a target it treats as symmetric: inflation that is too low is considered as undesirable as inflation that is too high. Actual inflation can differ from the target for long periods.

## Why inflation matters for investors

- **Cash and savings** lose purchasing power whenever their interest is below inflation (Category A).
- **Long horizons** magnify the effect: at 2% inflation per year, prices rise by about 49% over 20 years (1.02²⁰ ≈ 1.49).
- **Comparisons over time** should use real values. A share price that doubled over 30 years may have gained much less in real terms.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> €10,000 kept in cash for 20 years with no interest, while prices rise 2% a year, still shows €10,000 on the account, but buys only what about **€6,730** bought at the start (€10,000 ÷ 1.49).

> [!MISTAKE] "My account balance never went down, so I didn't lose anything"
> If your money earned less than inflation, you lost purchasing power, even though the number on your statement stayed the same or grew.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - Inflation reduces what money can buy.
> - Real return ≈ nominal return − inflation (exactly: (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1).
> - The ECB aims for 2% inflation over the medium term, measured by the HICP.
> - Over long periods, even modest inflation has a large effect on purchasing power.
