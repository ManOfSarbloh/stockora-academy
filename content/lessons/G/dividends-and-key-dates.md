A **dividend** is a payment a company makes to its shareholders, usually from its profits. In Austria, the general meeting decides on the use of the profit, including the dividend (Category A). Many companies pay once a year; some pay twice, quarterly, or not at all.

## The key dates

To know whether *you* get a dividend, you need four dates. The Vienna Stock Exchange describes them like this:

| Date | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cum date** | The last day on which you can buy the share **with** the right to the dividend |
| **Ex-date** (ex-dividend date) | The first day the share trades **without** the dividend |
| **Record date** | The day on which your holding in your securities account decides who receives the dividend |
| **Payment date** | The day the dividend is paid out |

Because trades settle a few days after they are made (Category D), you must **buy before the ex-date** to be a shareholder on the record date. In Vienna, the share must be bought at least two trading days before the record date (on the cum date at the latest).

> [!NOTE]
> In the US, where trades settle T+1, the ex-dividend date for ordinary cash dividends is usually the same as the record date. For very large dividends (25% or more of the share price), special rules apply and the ex-date is set after the payment.

## Why the price drops on the ex-date

On the ex-date, the company's value goes down by the cash it is about to pay out, so the share price is typically marked down by about the dividend amount. In the Vienna Stock Exchange's price lists, this is shown with the abbreviation "eD".

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A share closes at **€40.00** on the cum date. The dividend is **€1.20**.
>
> On the ex-date, the share opens at around **€38.80** (all other things equal). If you bought the day before, you own a share worth €38.80 plus a right to €1.20: still about €40 in total. The dividend did not create money out of nothing.

## Types of dividends

- **Ordinary (regular) dividends:** paid from profits on a regular schedule.
- **Special dividends:** one-off payments, for example after selling a business.
- **Stock dividends:** paid in additional shares instead of cash.

> [!MISTAKE] "I'll buy just before the ex-date to collect the dividend for free"
> The share price usually drops by about the dividend on the ex-date, so the value you hold stays about the same. On top of that, the dividend is usually taxed when it is paid, and you pay trading costs. There is no free money.

## Key takeaways

> [!TAKEAWAYS]
> - A dividend is a payment to shareholders; it is never guaranteed.
> - Buy before the ex-date to receive the dividend; the record date decides who is paid.
> - On the ex-date the share price usually falls by about the dividend amount.
> - Special dividends and stock dividends follow their own rules.
