## Which sources we use

We prefer sources in this order:

1. **Official sources:** stock exchanges, financial regulators, central banks, tax authorities, ministries and official statistics offices.
2. **Primary documents:** laws, company filings, prospectuses, annual reports.
3. **Academic research** and publications from recognised institutions.
4. **Reputable educational and news sources**, used for context, not as the only source for a fact.

## How we write

- All lessons are written in our own words. We do not copy text from books, websites or videos.
- All diagrams and illustrations are original.
- Every term is defined the first time it is used and linked to the glossary.
- Examples use simple, made-up numbers so you can follow the maths. They are not real prices or forecasts.
- If we cannot verify a fact, we either say so on the page or leave it out. We never guess numbers, dates, tax rates, hours or links.

## Translations

The site's original language is English. The interface, tools, glossary, Scam Shield and the information pages are also available in German, Spanish, French and Italian, translated with the help of AI; official names (such as KESt, FMA or source titles) are kept unchanged. Lessons, quiz questions and country pages exist in English only. If in doubt, the English version is the reference.

## Dates and time-sensitive facts

Every factual page shows a **last reviewed** date and lists its sources. Facts that change often (trading hours, holidays, tax rates, settlement rules, market sizes) are stored in data files with an "as of" date and are shown with a link to the official source so you can check them yourself.

## Videos

Videos are chosen for accuracy and neutrality, preferring educators, universities, public broadcasters, regulators and exchanges. We exclude channels that sell trading courses, signals or broker referrals. Video and source links are checked automatically every week, and broken ones are fixed or removed.
